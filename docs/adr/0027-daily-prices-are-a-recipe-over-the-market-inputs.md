# ADR 0027: A daily price is a recipe over the market inputs

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-30

The simulated Custodian needs one price per Security per Business day, the same every time for the same seed, Pin and date ([docs/product.md](../product.md), "The synthetic world"). [ADR 0019](0019-market-data-pinned-or-committed.md) fixed the inputs and [the Security catalog](../research/security-catalog.md) what drives each Security. This ADR fixes the recipe. Its evidence, with sources and what's computed, is in [docs/research/daily-prices.md](../research/daily-prices.md), section by section. #46 builds it. #47 shares points 1, 4, 5, 7 and 9.

1. **A Business day is a day the New York Stock Exchange is open.** Up to the Pin's last date it's a date in the Factor file. After it, it's a weekday that NYSE Rule 7.2 doesn't close, coded in the custodian and not taken from a dependency. The Treasury's dates aren't Business days: the bond market closes on Columbus and Veterans Day, and publishes on some days the NYSE is shut.
2. **A price starts on the first Business day on or after 2020-01-02,** the first date with yields (with the real Pin, 2020-01-02 itself). Each Security's price on it is a `base_price` in the catalog, an invented number, labelled as such in the catalog's research note. A price level changes no Return, only how much rounding matters. Every later price is built from the one before, so a date's price doesn't depend on when it was first worked out.
3. **Real history, then resampled days.** Up to the Pin's last date, a day's inputs are its own. A yield change is the yield minus the previous Business day's. A Business day with no yield keeps the last one, so its change is 0 and the next day's spans both; a yield published on a day that isn't a Business day counts only through the next Business day's change. Yields after the Pin's last date are ignored. After it, every resampled input (Mkt-RF, SMB, HML and the yield changes) comes from one source day in the pool: every Business day in the Factor file after the first one (1,673 days with the real Pin). Counting Business days after the Pin's last date from 0 as n, the block k is n ÷ 63 rounded down, its start is `between(stream("<seed>:block:<k>"), low=0, high=pool_size-1)`, and the source day is the one at index (start + (n mod 63)) mod pool_size of the pool, counting from 0. A block wraps around the pool's end.
4. **The skew of those years is taken out of resampled days only.** Mkt-RF, SMB and HML each get a constant per day added: the arithmetic mean of that column over every row of the Factor file, minus its mean over the pool, both as fractions. The tenors resampled are the 1-month and those the catalog names; a yield row with one of them empty is an error. Each has the pool's mean of its yield change subtracted, counting the 0-change days. Each level then starts at its yield on the Pin's last date and moves by that change, floored at zero every day. Whatever reads a yield or its change reads the floored level. Correcting the means is a choice of what the world should expect, not a measured fact: no source requires zero drift in yields. After the Pin's last date RF isn't resampled: it's the previous Business day's 1-month level × calendar days since then ÷ 365 (366 when the priced day's year is a leap year).
5. **A day's Return depends on what drives the Security.**
   - **Factors:** RF + each exposure × its Factor return + own surprise.
   - **Treasury:** −duration × the change in the level + the previous Business day's level × calendar days since then ÷ 365 (366 as above) + own surprise. The second term is the fund's **Carry**: the interest it earns just because time passes.
   - **Blend:** the factors part's weight × (RF + each exposure × its Factor return) + the Treasury part's weight × (−duration × the change + Carry) + one own surprise.
   - **None:** the Cash sweep stays $1.00, with no row in `price`.
6. **Own surprise** is one draw per Security per Business day, real or resampled, from `stream("<seed>:own:<date>:<identifier>")`, the date written `YYYY-MM-DD`, then capped at ±8 and × own volatility ÷ √252.
   - **A normal** comes from Marsaglia's polar method, which needs only arithmetic, `Decimal.sqrt` and `Decimal.ln`. Take two numbers u1 and u2 from `random()`, each turned into a `Decimal` exactly. With v1 = 2u1 − 1, v2 = 2u2 − 1 and s = v1² + v2², if s is above 0 and below 1 the normal is v1 × √(−2 ln s ÷ s) and v2 is discarded; otherwise take the next two numbers from the same stream.
   - **The draw** is a Student-t with 5 degrees of freedom, scaled to a spread of 1. It takes six normals in order: the first ÷ √(the sum of the next five squared ÷ 5), × √(3/5).
7. **One pinned `Decimal` context.** Every draw and price runs in an explicit `Context(prec=40, rounding=ROUND_HALF_EVEN)`, set in one place, never the global one. A different precision gives a different draw, so changing it is a rebuild of the world. This narrows [ADR 0013](0013-money-shares-prices-and-rates-are-separate-types.md)'s "full precision" for prices and draws, where a square root or logarithm has no exact answer.
8. **A price is kept to 4 decimal places, nearest, ties to even.** A day's price is the previous stored price × (1 + Return) − the dividend on an Ex-dividend date, rounded once. For example, $153.40 × (1 + 1.00%) = $154.9340. A price at or below zero stops the run with an error, never a clip.
9. **Income leaves as dividends.** A bond fund's dividend (#47) is the Carry it has accrued: each Business day's previous price × that day's Carry, added up over the days after its last Ex-dividend date, up to and including this one. A balanced fund's includes its Treasury part's weight × that. So price plus dividend grows at the Return, to within the rounding, and the price itself shows only the yield's moves and own surprise.
10. **The seed is recorded.** The market uses the world's seed, the one behind the world file (`DEFAULT_SEED`), and the build record keeps it, so a changed seed refuses to run on an old database as a changed Pin does. This extends [ADR 0019](0019-market-data-pinned-or-committed.md), point 5, whose record held only the inputs' hashes and the CRSP month.

## Why

- **The Factor file for the calendar, then the rule.** The rule agrees with the file on every weekday from 2020 to the Pin's last date except 2025-01-09, a one-off closure, and with NYSE's published 2026 to 2028 list. `exchange_calendars` agrees, but its default range moves with today's date and its data changes with releases, and a change of calendar moves every later block.
- **Blocks of 63.** Copying single days would lose how big moves cluster. The Politis-White length is 1 to 7 days for raw Factor returns and 2- to 20-year yield changes, and about 25 to 85 for their sizes, and 63 is a labelled choice inside that band. Fixed blocks have about 0.76 of the mean squared error of random-length ones (at each method's best length, for estimating a sample mean's variance), and need no scan back to find where a block starts.
- **Correct the means.** The pool's market return is 13.58% a year above RF against the whole file's 7.84%, and its yields rose about 0.43 points a year. Left in, an equity fund's median 10-year return is 18.5% a year against history's 10.7%, and the 20-year yield's median in 10 years is 9.9%. The target is a choice, not a fact: the pool's mean is only 0.71 standard errors from 7.84%. The Pin is the source because it needs nothing else to rebuild.
- **A floor at zero.** The Treasury floors its published par curve at zero. That rule is about its own curve, so using it here is a labelled choice. Without one, the demeaned 1-month yield goes below zero at some point in about 31% of 10-year paths.
- **Carry.** Rule 2a-4(a)(6) has a fund's NAV include income to date; Bloomberg's and the Treasury's accruals count calendar days. Against the published total returns of four real funds over 1, 3, 5 and 10 years, the recipe is within one point a year in 13 of 16 cells.
- **Student-t with 5.** Broad stock funds' residuals are near normal, industry portfolios sit near 5 degrees of freedom, and single stocks have a tail exponent near 3. It's a labelled simplification between them.
- **4 places.** No rule names the places for an ordinary fund, so this is a labelled choice. Cents cost a $10 bond fund a few percent over 1,673 days, because 19% of its days move less than half a cent; 4 places cost about 0.01%. Rounding down to cents drifts it by −78%, and down at 4 places by −0.8%.

## Considered options

- **Resampling single days, or random-length blocks.** Single days lose the clustering. Random-length blocks can be derived with no state, but cost a scan and more error.
- **Extending yields back before 2020.** A broader pool, but it reopens ADR 0019.
- **Mean reversion of yields.** It narrows the spread toward history, but no source gives its strength or target, and the pool can't estimate them.
- **A normal draw.** Nearer for broad funds, but it never makes a large surprise, and six of the catalog's Securities are single companies.
- **2 places, or 2 for stocks and 4 for funds.** Cents are too coarse for a fund near $10, and two rules need a catalog field for little gain.
- **The long-term fund at a duration of 16, or one that moves with yields.** The real fund moved about 15.99× the 20-year yield over 2016 to 2026, but 13.57 is the published number, and it's the current one. The gap stays in its own volatility of 0.025, as the catalog holds it.

## Consequences

- **Labelled simplifications** the docs must say: a random-walk yield curve spreads more than history, the pool is more volatile than the long run (Mkt-RF 20.8% a year against 17.1%, and the 2020 crash is 3% of its days), every bond fund follows one par yield, own surprise doesn't cluster, and in the file's years a stock fund's cash part is a staircase, because the file prints RF to 0.01% a day.
- **A Treasury fund's daily wobble is about half the real one's.** Own volatility was measured monthly, and the real funds' daily gaps partly reverse the next day.
- **Worked out day by day.** A block needs no state, but a yield level, a price and a bond fund's dividend each depend on the day before, so a call runs a loop from the first price. It takes about 6 seconds for 18 Securities over the real file's days since 2020.
- **The primer's Part 3 tables round to cents and its bond table has no Carry,** so #46 recomputes them.
- **The world's first Close is still #50's.** Prices start on the day in point 2 whichever day that is, so a later first Close reads a price already worked out.
- **The catalog changes,** with a `base_price` per priced Security, so its hash in the build record changes and existing databases are rebuilt. A number with no source is new to the catalog, so its docs and research note say so.
- **#47** floors the Sweep rate at zero itself, on top of the floored level, and pays dividends by point 9. Most worlds never see a 1-month yield at or below 0.5 points, but about one in ten spends a fifth of its days there.
