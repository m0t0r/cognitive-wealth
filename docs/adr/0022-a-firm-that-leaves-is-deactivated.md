# ADR 0022: A Firm that leaves is deactivated, not deleted

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-26

Since [ADR 0021](0021-firm-and-delivery-records-cant-be-deleted.md) a Firm with records can't be deleted, and until now nothing else existed for one that leaves: its members kept signing in and working on its records. An adviser that stops doing business must still "arrange for and be responsible for the preservation of" its books and records for the rest of their retention period, and tell the Commission where they're kept (Rule 204-2(f), [17 CFR 275.204-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section/275.204-2)); access to electronic records is limited to "properly authorized personnel and the Commission" (204-2(g)(3)(ii)). So leaving can't erase or orphan anything. A Firm that leaves, whether it stopped doing business or moved elsewhere, becomes a **Deactivated Firm** (`GLOSSARY.md`): its records stay as they are, and no User can act in it (#195).

1. **Only the operator deactivates.** `pnpm org:deactivate --slug <slug> --reason <text>` and `pnpm org:reactivate --slug <slug> --reason <text>`, beside `pnpm org:create`. An owner can't: leaving is an agreement between the Firm and Cognitive Wealth, not a button, and one click would lock every colleague out of a regulated record set. No browser endpoint is added (ADR 0004).
2. **`organization.deactivated_at`**, null while active. Who did it and why are in the audit event.
3. **Members are kept, and blocked.** Their `member` rows say who was on staff and in what role, history the retention rule wants kept, and keeping them is what makes reactivation free.
4. **Reactivation is allowed.** Nothing was changed, so there's nothing to undo; the irreversible step is destroying records past retention, which is #194. Invitations canceled at deactivation stay canceled.
5. **The tenant does the blocking.** `getViewer` ignores memberships in deactivated Firms, so `requireTenant` refuses one, the switcher hides it, a member of another Firm falls back to that one, and a User left with none lands on `/no-workspace`, as one removed from their only Firm does. Since `getViewer` reads memberships from Postgres on every request, the block holds from the next request on. Refusing at the sign-in eligibility check was rejected: that check runs in the `after()` task so nothing address-dependent shows to the requester (ADR 0006), a Firm-state rule there widens that path, and a User with no Firm can't see or do anything anyway.
6. **Nobody is signed out.** Revoking the sessions of members left with no Firm was considered and rejected: it adds no protection over point 5, and it would send them to a bare sign-in page that can't say why (point 10), so they'd learn what happened only after signing back in. Without it, their next click lands on the explanation. ADR 0005's rule (revoke through Better Auth, never SQL) is untouched, because nothing is revoked.
7. **Pending invitations into the Firm are canceled** in the deactivation's transaction, and accepting one into a deactivated Firm is refused, for the race.
8. **`withFirm` refuses a deactivated Firm**, so no domain path (the web app, `custodian:ingest`, `db:seed`, a future job) reads or writes its rows, whether or not its caller checked. Ingestion reports it as `refused` with reason `firm_deactivated`.
9. **Records are produced by the operator.** On request, the operator reads them as `migrator` (Drizzle Studio, SQL), whom row-level security doesn't restrict. A read-only role in the app was rejected: a third permission state across every page and Server Action, for a duty the rule lets the adviser arrange. An export CLI is #206.
   _Status note (2026-10-26):_ that CLI is `pnpm org:export --slug <slug> --out <dir> --reason <text>`, which writes a Records export (`GLOSSARY.md`) of an active or Deactivated Firm, read as `migrator` in one snapshot and audited as `org.records_exported` (`docs/auth.md`, "Records export").
10. **Users are told in the app, from the data.** `getViewer` reports the viewer's deactivated Firms on every request, and the pages show what that says. `/no-workspace` names the Firm that no longer uses Cognitive Wealth instead of "not in a workspace yet", for as long as it's the only kind the viewer has. A member who still has an active Firm sees a notice naming the deactivated one on every page until they dismiss it. Showing it only on the request that moved them off the Firm was rejected: that request may be a Server Action or a prefetch, which renders no page, so the notice would be lost. No email is sent: the Firm tells its own staff.
11. **A dismissal is a cookie, not a row.** Dismissing writes the Firm's `publicId` and its deactivation time into an `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Lax` cookie, and the notice hides when the cookie lists that pair. The pair, not the Firm alone, so a Firm deactivated again after a reactivation shows its notice again. A table would carry the dismissal across devices, but that's a new Firm-independent table, its privileges and its tests for a one-line notice. A cookie that's lost or edited only shows or hides the viewer's own notice, so it isn't signed.
12. **Audited** as `org.deactivated` and `org.reactivated`, with no actor (the operator isn't a User) and `metadata: { via: "cli", reason }`.

## Consequences

- A deactivated Firm's members keep valid sessions that reach nothing in it; they can still sign out, and accept an invitation into another Firm.
- A dismissal holds per browser: a second device, or cleared cookies, shows the notice again until it's dismissed there.
- A member moved off a Firm they were _removed_ from is still moved silently; telling them too is #207.
- Destroying a deactivated Firm's records past their retention period is #194.
