# Which Account roles make an Account part of a Household

> Research notes gathered on 2026-10-25 for the Household overview page (People, Accounts, AUM) and, later, AUM totals and fee householding. They inform the decision but are not a decision: the vocabulary is [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md), and where this note and an ADR disagree, the ADR wins. Every quote below was read on the cited page unless the text says otherwise. Typographic apostrophes are written straight.

The question: in Cognitive Wealth an Account isn't owned by a Household. People are linked to Accounts by dated Account roles (`owner`, `joint_owner`, `custodian`, `beneficiary`, `power_of_attorney`), and People are linked to Households by Household membership. Which roles pull an Account into a Household's overview, AUM and fee breakpoints?

## Answer first

1. **No source I read defines household membership as a list of Account roles.** The three places that say anything differ. Salesforce Financial Services Cloud derives the rollup from ownership: its client-level AUM rollups are keyed on the `PrimaryOwner__c` and `JointOwner__c` lookups, and its Trailhead text says "it rolls up to its primary owner and household". Tamarac (Envestnet) makes it an explicit, per-account Household membership with separate "AUM" and "Include in Net Worth" flags, so a Firm can list an account in a Household without counting it. Schwab's pricing guide links accounts by last name and home address, plus the customer's request. None of them says what a `beneficiary` or `power_of_attorney` role does to a rollup.
2. **Owner and joint owner count, and a joint account held by two members of one Household is counted once.** Salesforce: "the full balance appears in both Rachel and Nigel's profile. Because they're both in the Adams Household, however, the account balance is only counted once for the summary calculation." Tamarac: "AUM can only be enabled in one Household." ([Q1](#q1-owner-and-joint-owner))
3. **A beneficiary has no ownership or control while the owner is alive, so the role gives no claim on the Account's value.** Delaware's enactment of the uniform TOD act: the designation "has no effect on ownership until the owner's death", and the owner can change it "without the consent of the beneficiary". IRS Pub 590-A: a beneficiary receives the IRA's benefits "after the owner dies". The only aggregation rule I found that lets a beneficiary combine accounts is Capital Group's, and only after the trust's creator has died. ([Q2](#q2-beneficiary))
4. **For a UTMA/UGMA account the minor owns the property and the custodian only manages it.** The Maine UTMA: the transfer is irrevocable, the property "is indefeasibly vested in the minor", and the custodian has "all the rights, powers, duties and authority provided in this Act". No source says which Household a custodial account lands in when the minor and the custodian are in different ones. Schwab links custodial accounts by shared last name and address, and "in the same family" or "a dependent relationship" on request. ([Q3](#q3-custodian-for-a-minor))
5. **A power of attorney is an agent for the owner, not an owner.** The uniform Power of Attorney Act as enacted in Virginia defines an agent as someone "granted authority to act for a principal", and requires the agent to "act loyally for the principal's benefit". No source puts the agent's Household on the principal's account. ([Q4](#q4-power-of-attorney))
6. **For a 529 plan the account owner is the one who controls it and answers for it; the beneficiary is the student.** IRS: "Whoever purchases the 529 plan is the custodian and controls the funds until they are withdrawn"; "Otherwise, the account owner is considered the recipient of the distribution". Capital Group aggregates 529s "at the account owner level". ([Q5](#q5-529-plan-owner-or-beneficiary))
7. **Every fee-householding source I read leaves the definition of "household" to the firm or the fund.** FINRA: "Each mutual fund has different rules that govern when relatives may rely upon each other's holdings." The SEC's 2018 order against Retirement Capital Strategies faults a firm for not applying the household aggregation it had promised, and doesn't say who counts. The Firm's Advisory agreement must therefore say it. ([Q6](#q6-fee-householding))

## Q1. Owner and joint owner

**Salesforce Financial Services Cloud** is the closest match to this repo's model: Financial Account Roles link a Person to an account, and a Household groups the Persons.

- The role list. "Out of the box, Financial Services Cloud provides the following financial account roles. Primary Owner, Joint Owner, Beneficiary, Accountant, Trustee, Corporation, Foundation, Grantor, Business Manager." A role "describes the nature of a client's involvement with a financial account, such as joint owner, beneficiary, or trustee". ([Trailhead, "Explore Financial Account Roles and Financial Goals"](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/explore-financial-account-roles-and-financial-goals), read in the [Wayback copy of 2023-08-31](https://web.archive.org/web/20230831075026/https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/explore-financial-account-roles-and-financial-goals); the live page returns 404 now.)
- Owner roles are special. "primary and joint owners are deactivated by default and managed directly by Salesforce", every account needs at least a primary owner "even in the case of an individual account", and "you can't assign a joint owner to an account whose ownership type is Individual." (same page)
- The household rollup. "Whatever the type of account, it rolls up to its primary owner and household." And: "Because her account information is rolled up into her household, this new account also shows up in the Johnson Household." ([Trailhead, "Creating & Managing Financial Accounts Online"](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/view-financial-accounts), [Wayback copy of 2025-11-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20251111014222/https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/view-financial-accounts).)
- Joint accounts are counted once per Household. "if Rachel and her husband, Nigel, jointly owned an account ... the full balance appears in both Rachel and Nigel's profile. Because they're both in the Adams Household, however, the account balance is only counted once for the summary calculation." (same page)
- When owners are in different places, the primary owner decides. "If Rachel is the primary owner, the balance is included in her rollup summary. If the trust is the primary owner, the balance shows up in the trust's rollup summary and not in Rachel's." (same page)
- The client-level AUM rollups are keyed on the two owner roles. (The Household-level rollup uses "Group-level household lookup ... Lookup Field is set to Household__c" on the account, and the pages I read don't say how that field is set.) "Client-level lookup on Financial Account and Assets and Liabilities objects. Lookup Field is set to PrimaryOwner__c for the primary owner lookup and JointOwner__c for the joint owner lookup", and `RBLforAUMClientPrimaryOwner` "can only be triggered for managed financial accounts". ([Salesforce Help, "Rollup By Lookup Configuration and Criteria Fields (Managed Package)"](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?language=en_US&id=sf.fsc_admin_rbl_configurations.htm&type=5).)
- Membership decides whose accounts roll up. "The group is the member's primary group. This status defines which group the member's information is rolled up in. Each person can only have one primary group, and the person's information isn't rolled up in any other group. You can also choose which information rolls up to the group", and Financial Accounts is one of the choices. A business joins a group only when "The business is related to a person in the group" and "The business's Add to Group flag is set". ([Salesforce Help, "What Is a Group in Financial Services Cloud?"](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?language=en_US&id=sf.fsc_admin_data_model_group.htm&type=5).) This is the nearest thing to the "Include in Group" setting you asked about; I found no setting by that name.

**Tamarac Reporting (Envestnet)** makes the link explicit and separates "listed" from "counted".

- "Households group accounts, groups, and clients." ([Tamarac, "Learn More About Accounts, Groups, and Households"](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_reporting/accounts-groups-households/learn_more_about_accounts_groups_and_households.htm).)
- AUM is counted in one Household only. "AUM can only be selected for a Household membership. AUM can only be enabled in one Household." When it is on, "the account's assets under management will be included in managed value columns and household counts on compliance reporting". A 401(k) outside the Firm's management "would likely be included in a Household" yet "its assets won't be used in compliance reports". By default "Tamarac uses the first Household assigned to an account or group as the default primary Household." ([Tamarac, "Maintaining Accounts: The Household and Group Membership Panel"](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_reporting/accounts-groups-households/accounts/creating_and_maintaining_accounts/maintaining_accounts_the_household_and_group_membership_panel.htm).)
- Net worth has its own flag. "a child's parents may want to receive information about the child's trust account, so you'd want to include the account in the Household, but you'd exclude it from the net worth calculations since the account doesn't contribute towards the parents' financial goals." ([Tamarac, "Exclude an Account From Net Worth"](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_reporting/accounts-groups-households/accounts/exclude_an_account_from_net_worth.htm).)
- Ownership is a separate field. "Use this setting to designate the owner of a financial account, asset, or liability", and "You must set Primary Owner if the account's Ownership Type is Individual." ([Tamarac, "Primary Owner"](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/data_dictionary/p/primary_owner.htm).)

**Where the sources are silent or differ.**

- Salesforce's own standard example for "Roll Up Financial Accounts of a Household" joins Household, Account Contact Relation, Contact, Financial Account Party, Financial Account with no filter on the role field. My reading (an inference: the Financial Account Party object holds every role, per its [Data 360 DMO page](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/data/data-cloud-dmo-mapping/guide/c360dm-financial-account-party-dmo.html)) is that it would roll up an account where a member is only a Beneficiary or Trustee. The page doesn't say so either way. ([Salesforce Help, "Example: Roll Up Financial Accounts of a Household"](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=ind.fsc_admin_rollup_definitions_rollup_example.htm&language=en_US&type=5).) The managed-package AUM rule, by contrast, is keyed on the two owner roles. A Firm using Salesforce can get either result.
- Neither vendor says what happens when the owners of a joint account sit in two different Households. Salesforce's trust example (primary owner decides) and Tamarac's "one Household for AUM" point the same way, but neither states it for that case.
- The SEC's Form ADV is account-based, not household-based. Regulatory AUM is the "securities portfolios for which you provide continuous and regular supervisory or management services", and an account is a securities portfolio "if at least 50% of the total value of the account consists of securities". The word "household" doesn't appear in the instructions I searched; "Client" includes "family members of your supervised persons". ([SEC, Form ADV instructions, SEC 1707 (07-24)](https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/formadv-instructions.pdf), Item 5.b and the glossary.) So a Household AUM is the Firm's own measure, as [wealth-domain.md section 2.7](wealth-domain.md) already says.

## Q2. Beneficiary

**What a beneficiary has while the owner is alive: nothing that counts as ownership or control.**

- Transfer-on-death securities. "The designation of a TOD beneficiary on a registration in beneficiary form has no effect on ownership until the owner's death. A registration of a security in beneficiary form may be canceled or changed at any time by the sole owner or all then surviving owners without the consent of the beneficiary." ([12 Del. C. section 806](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c008/index.html), Delaware's enactment of the Uniform TOD Security Registration Act. I read only this state's text, not the Uniform Law Commission's.)
- IRAs. "An individual retirement account is a trust or custodial account set up in the United States for the exclusive benefit of you or your beneficiaries", and "A beneficiary can be any person or entity the owner chooses to receive the benefits of the IRA after the owner dies." ([IRS Publication 590-A (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a).)
- The one aggregation rule that touches a beneficiary: Capital Group lets investors combine "Trust accounts established by the above family members. If the person(s) who established the trust is deceased, the primary beneficiary can combine accounts." That is a beneficiary acting after the owner's death. ([Capital Group, "Reducing sales charges"](https://www.capitalgroup.com/individual/investments/share-class-information/reducing-sales-charges.html).)

**A spouse named as an IRA's beneficiary** is not an owner of that IRA, so the role alone doesn't make it part of the spouse's Household. In the usual case the spouse is in the same Household as the owner, so the IRA is there anyway through the `owner` role, and the `beneficiary` role is a fact about the Account (and the subject of the "no beneficiary" Signal in the glossary).

**An account in ANOTHER household naming a member** (a parent's IRA naming the child) is a different matter: the parent's Household owns it. No platform source I read pulls it into the child's Household. Salesforce's text says roles such as "beneficiary or trustee" exist, and keys its client-level AUM rollups on the owner roles. The Tamarac pages I read describe no role-based rule; membership is chosen. Wealth-domain.md already notes that "real families cross household lines" and that a separate party-to-party relationship graph is needed.

**A child as a 529 plan's beneficiary** is covered in [Q5](#q5-529-plan-owner-or-beneficiary).

## Q3. Custodian for a minor

- The minor owns it. "A transfer made pursuant to section 1660 is irrevocable and the custodial property is indefeasibly vested in the minor, but the custodian has all the rights, powers, duties and authority provided in this Act, and neither the minor nor the minor's legal representative has any right, power, duty or authority with respect to the custodial property, except as provided in this Act." ([33 M.R.S. section 1662](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/33/title33sec1662.html), Maine's enactment of the Uniform Transfers to Minors Act. Other states' enactments differ in details such as the termination age; I read only Maine's.)
- The custodian manages it for the minor. "A custodian may deliver or pay to the minor or expend for the minor's benefit as much of the custodial property as the custodian considers advisable for the use and benefit of the minor, without court order and without regard to the duty or ability of the custodian personally or of any other person to support the minor". ([33 M.R.S. section 1665](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/33/title33sec1665.html).)
- It ends in the minor's hands. The custodian "shall transfer in an appropriate manner the custodial property to the minor or to the minor's estate" on the minor reaching 18, or a later age (up to 21) if the transferor so specified. ([33 M.R.S. section 1671](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/33/title33sec1671.html).) State law sets the age (the glossary's "Custodial account" says so too).
- What platforms and custodians do. Schwab's pricing guide: "Schwab makes a reasonable effort to automatically link qualified accounts of persons with the same last name at the same home address", and "IRA, Custodial and certain Trust accounts that meet Schwab's householding guidelines generally will be automatically included in Accounts of Your Household and Household Calculations." For a fiduciary it adds that "it is your responsibility and not Schwab's to consider whether it is appropriate" to include the account. It will also link "if account holders are in the same family, there is a dependent relationship, or in certain other similar instances at Schwab's discretion." ([Schwab, Pricing Guide for Individual Investors](https://www.schwab.com/legal/schwab-pricing-guide-for-individual-investors), read 2026-10-25; this is a consumer brokerage guide whose "household" decides fee waivers, not an advisory fee schedule.) Capital Group combines "children under 21" with the parent's accounts for sales-charge reductions.
- Tamarac's example of a child's trust account is a case where the account is in the parents' Household but not in their net worth.

**What this says for the Household.** In the usual family case the minor (a Dependent) and the custodian (the Primary or Co-client) are in the same Household, so the account belongs there whichever role is followed. **No source says what to do when the custodian and the minor are in different Households** (a grandparent as custodian for a grandchild). I found no rule; treat it as a decision, not a finding.

## Q4. Power of attorney

- What it is. In the Uniform Power of Attorney Act as enacted in Virginia: "Agent" means "a person granted authority to act for a principal under a power of attorney", "Principal" means "an individual who grants authority to an agent in a power of attorney", and a power of attorney is "a writing or other record that grants authority to an agent to act in the place of the principal". ([Va. Code section 64.2-1600](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title64.2/chapter16/section64.2-1600/).) The agent must "Act loyally for the principal's benefit" and "Act only within the scope of authority granted in the power of attorney". ([Va. Code section 64.2-1612](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title64.2/chapter16/section64.2-1612/).) The agent acts for the principal and gets no title to the principal's property from the role.
- FINRA treats a power of attorney holder as a third party to the customer. Rule 4512 Supplementary Material .06(a) lets a firm contact the trusted contact person "to confirm the specifics of the customer's current contact information, health status, or the identity of any legal guardian, executor, trustee or holder of a power of attorney". ([FINRA Rule 4512](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4512).)
- No platform source I read rolls an account into the agent's Household. Trailhead's list of Salesforce's out-of-the-box roles (Primary Owner, Joint Owner, Beneficiary, Accountant, Trustee, Corporation, Foundation, Grantor, Business Manager) has no power-of-attorney role, but the managed package's `Role__c` picklist for an Account Role does include Power of Attorney and Custodian ([related-accounts-on-platforms.md](related-accounts-on-platforms.md)). Salesforce can record the role; nothing I read says it rolls an account into the agent's Household. Tamarac has no such role.

**An elderly parent in another Household** whose child holds the power of attorney: the account belongs to the parent's Household. The child (the agent) needs to see it, and the Firm's relationship with it runs through the principal. That "see it" is not "count it"; nothing I read says the agent's Household should include it in AUM.

## Q5. 529 plan: owner or beneficiary

- Control sits with the account owner. "Whoever purchases the 529 plan is the custodian and controls the funds until they are withdrawn." The designated beneficiary "is usually the student or future student for whom the plan is intended to provide benefits", and the owner can change it: "There are no tax consequences if you change the designated beneficiary to another member of the family." ([IRS, 529 Plans: Questions and Answers](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/529-plans-questions-and-answers).)
- The statute names only the beneficiary. "The term 'designated beneficiary' means the individual designated at the commencement of participation in the qualified tuition program as the beneficiary of amounts paid (or to be paid) to the program". ([26 U.S.C. section 529(e)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/529), read at Cornell LII because the official copy blocks WebFetch.) The IRS said as much about the owner: "The term 'account owner' does not appear in section 529 ... In practice, the AO retains control over the selection of the DB and has personal access to the funds in the account." ([IRS Announcement 2008-17](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/a2008_17.pdf).)
- Who is taxed. "The designated beneficiary is considered the recipient only if the distribution is made (a) directly to the designated beneficiary, or (b) to an eligible educational institution for the benefit of the designated beneficiary. Otherwise, the account owner is considered the recipient of the distribution." ([IRS Publication 970](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970), chapter on qualified tuition programs, the 2025 edition.)
- Who aggregates. Capital Group: "CollegeAmerica accounts, which will be aggregated at the account owner level". FINRA's FAQ lists "different types of accounts, such as 401(k)s and 529 plans" among those an investor may aggregate. ([FINRA, Frequently Asked Questions about Breakpoints](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/faqs/breakpoints-frequently-asked-questions).)

**So a 529 belongs to its owner's Household.** The beneficiary is a student who may be in a different Household (a grandparent funds a grandchild's plan, as wealth-domain.md notes). In the glossary's terms: the plan's `owner` role follows the owner, and the `beneficiary` role is the student.

## Q6. Fee householding

What the sources say about who may be aggregated for breakpoints:

- FINRA, on rights of accumulation: "A right of accumulation (ROA) allows investors to aggregate their own holdings as well as the holdings of certain related parties, such as spouses and children, toward achieving the investment thresholds at which breakpoint discounts become available." And: "Each mutual fund has different rules that govern when relatives may rely upon each other's holdings to qualify for breakpoint discounts." ([FAQ](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/faqs/breakpoints-frequently-asked-questions); [Breakpoints Disclosure Statement](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/breakpoints/disclosure-statement).) These are mutual fund sales charges, not advisory fees, and they decide the group by relationship, not by role on an account.
- Capital Group's list: "your spouse or equivalent (if recognized by law) and children under 21", with their IRAs, "Trust accounts established by the above family members", "Solely controlled business accounts", and CollegeAmerica accounts at the owner level. "Direct purchases of money market funds ... cannot be aggregated." ([Capital Group](https://www.capitalgroup.com/individual/investments/share-class-information/reducing-sales-charges.html).)
- Advisory fees. The SEC order against Retirement Capital Strategies found the firm "allowed related account balances of the same client and clients within the same household to be aggregated for the purposes of achieving the advisory fee breakpoint discounts", and that it "failed to take reasonable steps so that it consistently aggregated household accounts for all clients, as applicable", so 293 client accounts were overcharged about $304,000. ([SEC Release IA-5065, 2018-11-19](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2018/ia-5065.pdf).) It doesn't define "household". The SEC's 2018 risk alert, already cited in wealth-domain.md (section 2.8), lists failing to aggregate household accounts for breakpoints among the common findings; I didn't reread it.

**Sources disagree on the edges** (children's custodial accounts, trusts, entities, parents), and each says so: the fund, the firm or the custodian defines the group. So the Advisory agreement's definition of "household" has to be stored, and the role rule below is a default for it.

## Rule for Cognitive Wealth

Proposed rule. Each point says which finding it rests on, or that it is a design choice no source settles.

1. **An Account is part of a Household when a member of that Household holds an active `owner` or `joint_owner` role on it on the as-of date.** (Q1: Salesforce's client-level rollups are keyed on Primary and Joint Owner; Q3 and Q5: the legal owner of a UTMA/UGMA account is the minor, and of a 529 the account owner; this repo's `owner` plays Salesforce's Primary Owner.) Roles are dated, so the as-of date selects the roles.
2. **Count an Account once per Household,** however many of its owners are members. (Q1: Salesforce "only counted once for the summary calculation".)
3. **AUM, the Billable value and fee breakpoints include an Account in one Household only.** If its owners are in two Households, count it in the Household of the `owner` role and list it, uncounted, in the other. (Q1: Salesforce "the primary ownership determines where the account balance is included"; Tamarac "AUM can only be enabled in one Household". Neither states the two-Household case for a joint account, so this is a design choice that follows them.)
4. **`beneficiary` never makes an Account part of a Household.** If the beneficiary's Household isn't the owner's, list the Account under the beneficiary as a Named-on Account, with the role and the owning Household, and leave it out of the Account list's totals, AUM and fees. (Q2: no ownership or control while the owner is alive. Showing it at all is a design choice; Tamarac's "in the Household, out of net worth" shows the pattern, and listing it on the Person follows Salesforce's role list in [related-accounts-on-platforms.md](related-accounts-on-platforms.md). Built in #145.)
5. **`power_of_attorney` never makes an Account part of a Household.** Same display as `beneficiary`. (Q4: an agent acts for the principal and doesn't own.)
6. **`custodian` doesn't add an Account on its own.** A custodial account follows its owner, the minor, into the minor's Household, which is the custodian's too in the usual case. When the minor is in no Household (or a different one from the custodian's), list it under the custodian as a Named-on Account and keep it out of AUM until the Firm decides. (Q3: the minor owns it; no source covers the split case, so the second half is a design choice.)
7. **529 plans follow the `owner` role.** The student's `beneficiary` role doesn't pull the plan into the student's Household. (Q5.)
8. **Don't build a manual Household membership override yet.** Tamarac and Salesforce both let the user choose membership, and Schwab links on request, so one will probably be needed. The roles above give a default, and a per-Account override can be added when an issue asks for it. (Q1, Q6.)

**Open questions the sources don't settle** (ask the user or an ADR to decide): custodian and minor in different Households; joint owners in two Households; whether Household AUM should include a related Account when the Advisory agreement says so; what "household" means in the Fee schedule for trusts and entities. How platforms show and count the Accounts a member is only named on in another Household (Salesforce's role list, Orion's Associated Accounts, Tamarac's stakeholder Households) is in [related-accounts-on-platforms.md](related-accounts-on-platforms.md).
