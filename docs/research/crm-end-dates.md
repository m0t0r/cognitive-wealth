# What ends an Account role or a Household membership, and on what date

> Research notes gathered on 2026-10-27 for #192 (close CRM rows with an end date instead of deleting them), part of #189. They inform the decision but are not a decision: the vocabulary is [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md), and where this note and an ADR disagree, the ADR wins. Every quote below was read on the cited page unless the text says otherwise. Typographic apostrophes are written straight. Where a statute is quoted, the note says which state's enactment of the uniform act was read, because enactments differ.

The question: #192 wants `account_role` and `household_member` rows closed with an end date instead of deleted, and wants the dates to mean what a real Custodian means by them. What ends each Account role (owner, joint owner, custodian for a minor, beneficiary, power of attorney) and on what date does the end take effect? What do advisor CRMs do with a Person who leaves a Household? And is a Prospect who never became a Client a record the Firm must keep under SEC Rule 204-2?

## Answer first

1. **For a sole owner with a TOD registration, death moves ownership on the date of death; the Custodian re-registers later, on proof and papers.** Delaware's TOD act, which covers only "securities registered in beneficiary form": ownership "passes to the beneficiary" on death, and the security "may be reregistered" on "proof of death ... and compliance with any applicable requirements of the registering entity". Without a TOD registration the account goes through the estate (Fidelity asks for a death certificate and a court appointment or small-estate affidavit), and the sources I read don't give a date on which the decedent's ownership ends. ([Q1](#q1-what-ends-each-account-role))
2. **Joint owner: with survivorship only the decedent's role ends; tenants in common and community property send the share to the estate.** Janus Henderson: "the entire account passes to the survivor(s)" versus "that portion of the account passes to their estate". A divorce decree doesn't re-title the Account in these sources: FINRA says you "typically must notify the financial institution in writing" to close the joint account and open separate ones. ([Q1](#q1-what-ends-each-account-role))
3. **Power of attorney ends on the event, but a good-faith actor without "actual knowledge" is protected, and Fidelity's form ends it on notice.** Virginia 64.2-1608(D); Fidelity: "in full force and effect, until Fidelity has received ... satisfactory written notice". It needs two dates: the event, and when the Custodian learned. ([Q1](#q1-what-ends-each-account-role))
4. **Custodian for a minor ends at the state's age (Maine 18 to 21 for most transfers; Virginia 18, 21 or 25, with the minor able to demand the property at 21 when it's 25) or the minor's death.** A resigning custodian gives notice and delivers "the custodial property" to a successor; Fidelity requires "the same state and age of termination". ([Q1](#q1-what-ends-each-account-role))
5. **Beneficiary: the owner may change it "at any time ... without the consent of the beneficiary"; at death it becomes ownership.** A spouse who inherits a traditional IRA may "Treat it as your own IRA by designating yourself as the account owner"; anyone else "can't treat the inherited IRA as your own", and may move it by trustee-to-trustee transfer only to an IRA kept "in the name of the deceased IRA owner for the benefit of you as beneficiary". ([Q1](#q1-what-ends-each-account-role))
6. **Same Account or new number is Fidelity's rule:** same primary owner, "Account number will stay the same. Exception: UTMAs/UGMAs"; new primary owner, "Account number will change." An estate transfer closes the old account. ([Q1](#q1-what-ends-each-account-role))
7. **CRMs differ:** Wealthbox keeps a deceased Person in the Household and puts the death on the Person; Redtail and Tamarac CRM remove them and relate them instead (Redtail a "Deceased Husband/Wife/Spouse" membership, Tamarac a "Deceased Spouse" connection), and all three record a date of death on the Person; Wealthbox takes a Person out of the old Household when added to a new one and says nothing on history. ([Q2](#q2-when-a-person-leaves-a-household))
8. **Rule 204-2 lists no Prospect record, but keeps written communications about advice "proposed to be given" (a)(7), and limits the brochure-date record (a)(14) to prospects "who subsequently becomes a client".** The clock is five years from the end of the fiscal year of the last entry on each record, (e)(1), except advertisements (a)(11), whose clock runs from when they were last disseminated, (e)(3)(i). Reg S-P makes a person seeking advice a consumer but requires no retention. ([Q3](#q3-is-a-prospect-who-never-became-a-client-a-record-under-rule-204-2))

## Q1. What ends each Account role

The Custodian is the book of record for the Account and its registration (GLOSSARY.md, "Book of record"); the Firm's Account roles are the Firm's own dated facts about People. So the sources below are the Custodian's forms and the statutes those forms rest on. **None of the pages I read says what dates a Custodian's own records or data feed carry for a death or a re-registration.** What they show is the paperwork: the claim forms collect the date of death as data, and the registration changes when the claim is processed.

### Owner (sole), on death

- With a TOD registration, ownership changes at death. Delaware's chapter covers only "securities registered in beneficiary form"; for a sole owner without one, the account goes to the estate (Fidelity's form, below), and none of the sources I read says on what date the decedent's ownership ends in that case. "On death of a sole owner or the last to die of all multiple owners, ownership of securities registered in beneficiary form passes to the beneficiary or beneficiaries who survive all owners. On proof of death of all owners and compliance with any applicable requirements of the registering entity, a security registered in beneficiary form may be reregistered in the name of the beneficiary or beneficiaries who survived the death of all owners." And: "If no beneficiary survives the death of all owners, the security belongs to the estate of the deceased sole owner". ([12 Del. C. section 807](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c008/index.html), Delaware's enactment of the Uniform TOD Security Registration Act; I read only this state's text.) The two sentences carry two dates: ownership passes "on death" (Valid time), the registration changes after "proof of death" and the registering entity's requirements (the Custodian's Record time).
- The registering entity is protected when it acts on what it was told. "A registering entity is discharged from all claims to a security by the estate, creditors, heirs or devisees of a deceased owner if it registers a transfer of the security in accordance with section 807 of this title and does so in good faith reliance ... on information provided to it by affidavit of the personal representative of the deceased owner, or by the surviving beneficiary". ([12 Del. C. section 808(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c008/index.html).) The protection has a limit: it does "not extend to a reregistration or payment made after a registering entity has received written notice from any claimant to any interest in the security objecting to implementation" (same chapter).
- Fidelity, an individual account with the owner deceased: "Probated estate: Legal representative signs ... Non-probated estate: New owner signs". Required documents: "Copy of death certificate", "Certified copy of court appointment naming executor of the estate dated within 180 days or a small estate affidavit or alternate court document if eligible under state law", and "Copy of state tax waiver, if required by your state". It also warns: "ALL required forms and supporting documentation must be provided at the time this form is submitted, or we will be unable to reregister the account as requested." ([Fidelity, "Change of Account Registration - Information"](https://fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/customer-service/CHANGE_BROKERAGE.pdf), pages 1 to 3 of the form, read 2026-10-27.)
- Fidelity's separate form for an estate with no probate (the account "Must have a value of $25,000 or less" and "be eligible for a nonprobate transfer under the law of the decedent's state, and the decedent's state cannot have small estate provisions") asks for the "Date of Death", and says: "The deceased owner's account will be closed once all assets have been transferred." ([Fidelity, "Transfer Ownership - Nonprobate Estate - Nonretirement"](https://www.fidelity.com/accounts/pdf/NonretireNonprobate.pdf); WebFetch saved the PDF and I extracted it with pypdf, because `curl` got a 403.)
- Vanguard: "Gather what you'll need: Decedent social security number, date of birth, and date of passing. Copy of death certificate (we'll need this if we can't verify the decedent's passing on our end)." After the request: "Vanguard will: Freeze the account of the person who passed away to protect it from unauthorized activity." For an executor: "Executors must provide court documentation, such as Letters Testamentary, Letters of Administration, or a short certificate." Where assets go: "Assets covered by a TOD Plan generally transfer directly to an account in the beneficiary's name. Assets that pass through the estate may move to an estate account before distribution." ([Vanguard, "Inheriting a Vanguard account"](https://investor.vanguard.com/inherit/faq); [Vanguard, executor's guide](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/education/helping-executor-through-ira-inheritance-process).)
- Schwab's executor sheet: "Certified copies of your Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration from the appropriate probate court, dated within 12 months", a Tax Identification Number for the estate ("Do not use the decedent's Social Security number"), and "Prompt transfer of title--Schwab can streamline the process of registering the decedent's securities in the name of the estate or transferring title of the underlying assets in the account to the heirs." ([Schwab Estate Services, SLS112343](https://content.schwab.com/dcc/SLS112343.pdf).)
- A Custodian can change an Account's identifiers when someone dies. Tamarac (a reporting platform, not a Custodian): "When a client passes away, the custodian may move the deceased client's account to a different master account, g number, or rep code. If this happens, Tamarac may lose the data connection." ([Tamarac, "Best Practices for When a Client Passes Away"](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_reporting/user_security_and_system_settings/best_practices_for_when_a_client_passes_away.htm).)

**What this says for an owner's death.** With a TOD registration, the sole owner's role ends on the date of death, which is when ownership passes (Delaware 807). Without one, the sources name no date; the date of death is the natural Valid time, but that is an inference, not a finding. The Custodian will not show it until it has proof of death and, for an estate, court papers, so the Firm will often learn of a death before the Account is re-registered and sometimes after. The new holder is the beneficiary, or the estate, which is not a Person in the schema (an open question, below).

### Joint owner

- Janus Henderson, a mutual fund company (not a Custodian), describes the registrations. Joint tenants with rights of survivorship: "Upon the death of one owner, the entire account passes to the survivor(s)." Community property: "In the event of death, the decedent's portion passes to their estate, rather than directly to the surviving spouse." Tenants in common: "Upon the death of one owner, that portion of the account passes to their estate." ([Janus Henderson, "Joint Account"](https://www.janushenderson.com/en-us/investor/resources/planning/all-account-types/joint-account).) I did not find a state statute on survivorship in securities accounts; this page and Fidelity's form (next point) are the sources.
- Fidelity's form follows the same split. "Joint Account - Replacing a Deceased Owner JWROS or Tenants by Entirety": signed by the "Surviving current owner(s)", with a "Copy of death certificate" and a state tax waiver "if required". "... Tenants in Common or Community Property" adds the "Certified copy of court appointment naming executor of the estate", and a "Letter of Instruction confirming ownership share if needed for clarification". (Fidelity form, page 2.)
- Divorce. FINRA: "For taxable accounts, such as a brokerage account you own jointly with your spouse, you typically must notify the financial institution in writing to request that the joint account be closed and that new, separate accounts be opened in each person's name. The letter should detail how to allocate the investment assets between the two accounts." And on beneficiaries: "In many cases, a former spouse who is still listed as a beneficiary will receive the funds from those accounts upon your death. While some states have statutes that automatically revoke the beneficiary status of an ex-spouse, it's best to address this yourself". ([FINRA, "6 Tips for Managing Investments Through Divorce"](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/6-tips-managing-your-investments-through-divorce), read in a headed browser because `curl` got a 403.) Fidelity's removal of an owner needs "All current owners must be living" and the current owners' signatures (form, page 1). The pages I read name no Custodian rule for a divorce decree.

**What this says for a joint owner.** With survivorship the decedent's `joint_owner` role ends on the date of death and the survivor's role continues, though the Custodian re-registers the Account only when the survivor files the form. With tenants in common the decedent's role ends the same way, but the share goes to the estate. A divorce ends nothing on its own date: the roles end when the joint Account is closed or re-titled, and that happens on a request, so the date is a Custodian event, not the decree's date. A former spouse's `beneficiary` role may be revoked by state law (FINRA: "some states have statutes that automatically revoke the beneficiary status of an ex-spouse"; it doesn't say from what date) while the Custodian's record still shows it.

### Power of attorney

The Uniform Power of Attorney Act (UPOAA), as enacted in Virginia. I read this state's text only. Virginia's section numbers are 64.2-1608 (termination) and 64.2-1617 (reliance); the uniform act's section numbers for the same rules, 110 and 119, come from the task brief and I did not open the Uniform Law Commission's text.

- What ends it. "A. A power of attorney terminates when: 1. The principal dies; 2. The principal becomes a vulnerable adult, if the power of attorney is not durable; 3. The principal revokes the power of attorney; 4. The power of attorney provides that it terminates; 5. The purpose of the power of attorney is accomplished; or 6. The principal revokes the agent's authority or the agent dies, becomes a vulnerable adult, or resigns, and the power of attorney does not provide for another agent". "B. An agent's authority terminates when: 1. The principal revokes the authority; 2. The agent dies, becomes a vulnerable adult, or resigns; 3. Unless the power of attorney otherwise provides, an action is filed (i) for the divorce or annulment of the agent's marriage to the principal or their legal separation, (ii) by either the agent or principal for separate maintenance from the other, or (iii) by either the agent or principal for custody or visitation of a child in common with the other; 4. The agent is convicted of financial exploitation of the principal under section 18.2-178.2; or 5. The power of attorney terminates." ([Va. Code section 64.2-1608](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title64.2/chapter16/section64.2-1608/).) Two details to notice: Virginia says "vulnerable adult" here, though its own 64.2-1602 and 64.2-1616 still say "incapacity" and "incapacitated", and the divorce trigger is the filing of an action, not the decree.
- Durable by default. "A power of attorney created under this chapter is durable unless it expressly provides that it is terminated by the incapacity of the principal." ([Va. Code section 64.2-1602](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title64.2/chapter16/section64.2-1602/).)
- The good-faith rule. "D. Termination of an agent's authority or of a power of attorney is not effective as to the agent or another person that, without actual knowledge of the termination, acts in good faith under the power of attorney. An act so performed, unless otherwise invalid or unenforceable, binds the principal and the principal's successors in interest. E. Incapacity of the principal of a power of attorney that is not durable does not revoke or terminate the power of attorney as to an agent or other person that, without actual knowledge of the incapacity, acts in good faith under the power of attorney." (Va. Code section 64.2-1608.) And for a person who accepts a notarized power: "A person that in good faith accepts an acknowledged power of attorney ... without actual knowledge that the power of attorney is void, invalid, or terminated, that the purported agent's authority is void, invalid, or terminated, or that the agent is exceeding or improperly exercising the agent's authority may rely upon the power of attorney as if the power of attorney were genuine, valid, and still in effect". The same section: an institution "that conducts activities through employees and exercises commercially reasonable procedures to communicate information concerning powers of attorney among its employees is without actual knowledge of a fact ... if the employee conducting the transaction ... has followed such procedures and is nonetheless without actual knowledge of the fact." ([Va. Code section 64.2-1617](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title64.2/chapter16/section64.2-1617/), subsections B and F.)
- The Custodian's own contract ends it on notice. Fidelity's durable power of attorney form, signed by the agent: "Agree to serve as Attorney-in-Fact, and acknowledge that this POA remains in full force and effect, until Fidelity has received what it considers to be satisfactory written notice of either the account owner's death or your removal or resignation as Attorney-in-Fact." The owner agrees to indemnify Fidelity for the agent's actions "even if your Attorney-in-Fact's authorization has been terminated but Fidelity has not yet received notice of the termination", and that "revocation or termination hereof shall be ineffective as to Third Parties, unless and until actual written notice of such revocation or termination shall have been received by such Third Parties." To revoke: "submit a new POA form or a letter of instruction (LOI)". ([Fidelity, "Durable Power of Attorney - Requirements"](https://www.fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/customer-service/durable-power-of-attorney.pdf), read 2026-10-27.)
- Vanguard does not use the outside document for a living, capable owner. "If you're able to manage your own financial affairs (and are not incapacitated), Vanguard won't accept a DPOA to establish ongoing access to your accounts. If you wish to establish ongoing full authority, you'll need to complete a Full Agent Authorization form." And "A general, non-durable, power of attorney stops upon incapacity, whereas Vanguard's full agent authorization continues when the account owner becomes incapacitated." ([Vanguard, "Authorized account access"](https://investor.vanguard.com/client/authorized-account-access).) So a Custodian's "agent" can be its own grant, with its own end rules, not the statutory power.

**What this says for a power of attorney.** The statute puts the end on the event: the principal's death, the revocation, the agent's death or resignation, the filing of a divorce. But the same statute makes the end ineffective for anyone who acts in good faith without actual knowledge, and Fidelity's form makes it end, as far as Fidelity is concerned, at notice. So the single date `ends_on` can't answer "was this agent authorized when this order was placed?" on its own: that needs the event date and the date the Custodian (or the Firm) learned of it. The Record time of closing the role is the second date; ADR 0025's history table keeps it. Whether a Firm that learned of a death is itself "without actual knowledge" is the Firm's question, not the Custodian's, and the sources only say the Custodian is protected until it has notice.

### Custodian for a minor (UTMA/UGMA)

- The age is the state's. Maine: the custodian "shall transfer in an appropriate manner the custodial property to the minor or to the minor's estate upon the earlier of: 1. ... The minor's attainment of 18 years of age with respect to custodial property transferred under section 1655 or 1656, unless the transferor has specified in writing in the transferring instrument that the transfer shall not occur until the minor attains a later age, not to exceed 21 years of age ... 3. ... The minor's death." ([33 M.R.S. section 1671](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/33/title33sec1671.html), Maine's enactment of the Uniform Transfers to Minors Act.) The 18-to-21 range is for transfers under sections 1655 and 1656; for property transferred under 1657 or 1658 the age is the minor's "majority under the laws of this State other than this Act". Virginia's differs: "the minor's attainment of 18 years of age or if the transfer was made as provided in subsection D or E of section 64.2-1908, the minor's attainment of 21 or 25 years of age, as applicable; or ... The minor's death." ([Va. Code section 64.2-1919](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title64.2/chapter19/section64.2-1919/).) Where the age is 25, subsection B lets the minor demand the property at 21, so the role can end earlier than the registered age.
- A custodian resigns by delivering things, not only a notice. Maine: "A custodian may resign at any time by delivering written notice of resignation to the minor, if the minor has attained 14 years of age, and to the successor custodian and by delivering the custodial property to the successor custodian." If the custodian "dies or becomes incapacitated without having effectively designated a successor", the minor (if 14 or older) or the minor's conservator picks one. ([33 M.R.S. section 1669](https://www.mainelegislature.org/legis/statutes/33/title33sec1669.html).)
- At the Custodian. Fidelity: "UGMA/UTMA Account with Different Custodian. Note: The same state and age of termination as existing UTMA/UGMA account must be kept." The resigning custodian signs; for a custodian who died, a successor custodian signs with the death certificate and other papers that depend on the minor's age ("Minor under 14", "between 14-20", "Former minor 21 and over: New owner (former minor)"). The new-registration form's default state: "minor's state of residence". (Fidelity form, pages 1 to 3 and Section 3.)

**What this says for a custodian.** The `custodian` role ends on the earliest of the termination age, the minor's death, and a resignation or removal. The first can be computed only if the Firm knows the state and the age the transferor chose, and `person.born_on` alone doesn't give that; the Custodian's registration holds it. A successor custodian is a new `custodian` role starting the day the old one ends. When the age is reached, the minor becomes the owner: an `owner` role starts as the `custodian` role ends. The duty is to "transfer", so the Custodian's re-registration lags the age by however long the transfer takes.

### Beneficiary

- The owner controls it. "The designation of a TOD beneficiary on a registration in beneficiary form has no effect on ownership until the owner's death. A registration of a security in beneficiary form may be canceled or changed at any time by the sole owner or all then surviving owners without the consent of the beneficiary." ([12 Del. C. section 806](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title12/c008/index.html).) At death see section 807, above: ownership "passes to the beneficiary or beneficiaries who survive all owners", who "hold their interests as tenants in common" until division.
- IRAs, the spouse. "If you inherit a traditional IRA from your spouse, you generally have the following two choices. Treat it as your own IRA by designating yourself as the account owner; or Treat it as your own by rolling it over into your IRA ... or Treat yourself as the beneficiary rather than treating the IRA as your own." The choice can be made by conduct: "You will be considered to have chosen to treat the IRA as your own if: Contributions (including rollover contributions) are made to the inherited IRA, or You don't take the required minimum distribution for a year as a beneficiary of the IRA." But only for a sole beneficiary: "You will only be considered to have chosen to treat the IRA as your own if: You are the sole beneficiary of the IRA, and You have an unlimited right to withdraw amounts from it." Anyone else: "you can't treat the inherited IRA as your own", and "you can make a trustee-to-trustee transfer as long as the IRA into which amounts are being moved is set up and maintained in the name of the deceased IRA owner for the benefit of you as beneficiary". That's a condition on where a transfer may go, not a rule about how the inherited Account itself is titled. All of this is the publication's text for a traditional IRA ("If you inherit a traditional IRA"). ([IRS Publication 590-B (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b), "What if You Inherit an IRA?".) Vanguard: "Assets typically move to an inherited IRA or are distributed according to applicable beneficiary rules."
- A beneficiary who is a 529 plan's student is a different thing (see [household-accounts.md, Q5](household-accounts.md#q5-529-plan-owner-or-beneficiary)); the owner changes them.

**What this says for a beneficiary.** A `beneficiary` role ends when the owner changes the designation (the date the Custodian records the change, since it takes effect by the owner's act) or at the owner's death, and at death it is replaced by ownership: the beneficiary's `beneficiary` role ends and an `owner` role starts on the Account, or on a new inherited Account. A spouse who is the sole beneficiary can keep the same Account by designating themself owner. A non-spouse can't treat it as their own; if they move it by trustee-to-trustee transfer, the receiving IRA must be in the decedent's name for their benefit, and Vanguard calls it "an inherited IRA"; whether that is a new Account number the sources don't say, though Fidelity's rule is that a changed primary owner gets one (below).

### Re-registration: same Account or new number

- Fidelity's form, Section 2 and the opening text. Keep the primary owner: "Tax reporting number for the account remains the same. Account number will stay the same. Exception: UTMAs/UGMAs require a new account number even if the tax reporting number remains the same." Change the primary owner: "Tax reporting number for the account is changing. Account number will change." The signer acknowledges that "in certain circumstances a new account number may be assigned", and "by changing the registration, you may be changing the ownership interests and/or removing individuals authorized to transact business or access this account." If there is a new account, features "will not carry over from the existing account". (Fidelity form, "Helpful to Know", Section 2 and Section 8.)
- Adding or removing a joint owner (primary owner kept) therefore usually keeps the number. Replacing the primary owner, or any UTMA/UGMA registration change, gives a new one. An estate-transfer or inherited IRA is a new account in a new name (above).
- Rollovers and trustee-to-trustee transfers move money between accounts. Pub 590-B: "you can make a trustee-to-trustee transfer as long as the IRA into which amounts are being moved is set up and maintained in the name of the deceased IRA owner". I read no page that calls a rollover a re-registration; the glossary's "Rollover" is "Moving retirement money from one plan or IRA to another", i.e. two Accounts.

**What this says for re-titling.** Whether the Account row survives depends on one fact the Custodian holds and the CRM doesn't: whether the primary owner changed. If it didn't, the Account keeps its number and the roles around it change (a `joint_owner` ends, an `owner` starts). If it did, the old Account ends in the Custodian's feed and a new one starts, and the Firm's roles must end on the old row and start on the new one on the same date.

## Q2. When a Person leaves a Household

Not one of these four sources lists a membership end date as a field, except where noted. What each does:

- **Wealthbox**, a deceased person in a Household: "We recommend keeping the deceased individual linked to their Household record as a family member. However, mark their personal contact record as Inactive. This approach preserves historical activity and maintains the integrity of the Household's relationship history, while clearly indicating the individual is no longer active." And for the person: "add the individual's Date of Death" under "Special Dates", then "change the contact record's 'Status' field to 'Inactive.'" ([Wealthbox, "What is a Contact in Wealthbox"](https://help.wealthbox.com/hc/en-us/articles/29980435004059-What-is-a-Contact-in-Wealthbox-and-what-are-common-questions), read in a headed browser.) Moving: "No, a person contact can only be associated with one household at a time in Wealthbox. If a person is added to a new household while already related to another, they will be automatically removed from the previous household and reassigned to the new one." "Related contacts can be used to relate contact records that are not under the same address. For example, adult children." ([Wealthbox, "What are Household Records and their best practices?"](https://help.wealthbox.com/hc/en-us/articles/29980360361755-What-are-Household-Records-and-their-best-practices).) Its contact types, from the first page: "Prospect, Client, Past Client, Vendor, or Organization".
- **Tamarac CRM**, a client who dies (the page is about an account's contacts; Tamarac Reporting's "Household" below is a group of accounts, not of people): on the contact, "In the Deceased Date box, type the date the client passed away." On the account, remove the deceased: "Click the X. If deleting the primary contact, replace the deleted contact with another name." Then add a Connection to the deceased with the role "Deceased Spouse", and "Create a task to deactivate the deceased contact at the end of the year. The contact needs to remain active through the current year to maintain any RMD requirements you are tracking on the RMD report." Then "Adding a connection between the account and the deceased's contact allows you to keep the history for this client and deactivate the deceased's contact keeping your system streamlined without losing vital information." ([Tamarac CRM, "Managing the Passing Away of a Client"](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_crm/client_offboarding/managing_the_passing_away_of_a_client.htm).)
- **Tamarac Reporting**, the accounts: "Close the financial account. Enter a Closed date for the date you stopped managing the account. We recommend that you do not remove the account from the reporting group. The account's continued presence will provide important context for performance and transaction data." And a warning about removal: "If you remove an account from an account set, it will be excluded from any reports run on historical date periods, as well as from future reports." If the assets leave, "Mark the Household with a ZZ prefix in the Household Name to indicate the Household is no longer active." (Tamarac Reporting, "Best Practices for When a Client Passes Away", linked above.) Its Remove button has no date: "Click Remove next to each direct member you want to remove." ([Tamarac, "Add or Remove Members of a Household"](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_reporting/accounts-groups-households/households/creating_and_maintaining_households/add_or_remove_members_of_a_household.htm).)
- **Redtail**, a client who dies: "Create a Contact Status of 'Deceased'", "Fill out the 'Date of Death' field on the client's contact record", "Update the spouse's record to designate them as the new head of household", "Add a membership option for 'Deceased Husband/Wife/Spouse'", and "Remove the deceased from the family, and add memberships between the deceased and living relatives. This will allow for the relationship to be tracked without infringing on the tax definition of family." ([Redtail, "Tips from Redtail's Training Team: How to Handle ... Client Death"](https://corporate.redtailtechnology.com/tips-from-redtails-training-team-how-to-handle-client-death/), 2022-05-26.)
- **Salesforce Financial Services Cloud**: "Group membership is modeled using the Account Contact Relationship object." Each person has one primary group: "the person's information isn't rolled up in any other group." ([Salesforce Help, "What Is a Group in Financial Services Cloud?"](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?language=en_US&id=sf.fsc_admin_data_model_group.htm&type=5), read in a headed browser.) The page says nothing about ending a membership; I could not open the object reference that would list an end-date field (see below).

**What this says for leaving a Household.** Wealthbox keeps the person in the Household and marks the Person (a date of death, an Inactive status); Redtail and Tamarac CRM remove them and relate them instead (a "Deceased Husband/Wife/Spouse" membership, a "Deceased Spouse" connection); Wealthbox's move to a second Household takes them out of the first and says nothing about history. All three give the death its own date on the Person (Tamarac's Deceased Date, Wealthbox's Date of Death, Redtail's Date of Death), not on the Household membership. A Dependent who grows up has no stated rule in these sources: Wealthbox offers "Related contacts" as an option ("For example, adult children"), and adding a Person to a second Household takes them out of the first. Tamarac Reporting's warning is about removing an account from an account set, not a Person from a Household, but it's the analogous risk for #192: removing a row changes what a report for a past date shows.

## Q3. Is a Prospect who never became a Client a record under Rule 204-2

I read 17 CFR 275.204-2 on eCFR (the current text through the versioner API of 2026-10-20; (a)(25) is absent from eCFR's 2024-06-03 version of the section and present from its 2024-08-02 version, the Reg S-P amendments) and Part 248 the same way.

- **(a)(7)** covers "Originals of all written communications received and copies of all written communications sent by such investment adviser relating to: (i) Any recommendation made or proposed to be made and any advice given or proposed to be given; (ii) Any receipt, disbursement or delivery of funds or securities; (iii) The placing or execution of any order to purchase or sell any security ...". The text does not say "client": it covers communications "relating to" advice "proposed to be given", which is what a proposal to a Prospect is. I found no SEC statement that applies or disapplies it to prospects. The Marketing Rule adopting release records an adviser-side practice: "most advisers have developed procedures requiring the retention of all written communications, so that individuals within the firm do not have the discretion to determine whether or not a particular communication is required under rule 204-2(a)(7)." (Release No. IA-5653, footnote 809, a commenter's letter quoted by the SEC, not the SEC's own view.)
- **(a)(11)** covers "A copy of each ... Advertisement (as defined in section 275.206(4)-1(e)(1))" disseminated, and any "communication that the investment adviser disseminates ... to ten or more persons". The adopting release says the definition's first prong "will not include one-on-one communications, unless the communication includes hypothetical performance information that is not provided: (i) in response to an unsolicited investor request or (ii) to a private fund investor", and that excluding them avoids impeding "typical communications between advisers and their existing and prospective investors". So a one-to-one pitch to one Prospect is not an advertisement under the first prong unless it carries hypothetical performance (outside those two cases), and a brochure or deck sent to many Prospects is. Retention for (a)(11) runs "from the end of the fiscal year during which the investment adviser last published or otherwise disseminated" it ((e)(3)(i)).
- **(a)(14)(i)** covers each brochure, brochure supplement and Form CRS "and a record of the dates that each brochure, brochure supplement and Form CRS, each amendment or revision thereto, and each summary of material changes not contained in a brochure given to any client or to any prospective client who subsequently becomes a client." A Prospect who never became a Client is outside the date record by its words.
- **(a)(10)**: "All written agreements (or copies thereof) entered into by the investment adviser with any client or otherwise relating to the business of such investment adviser as such." A signed document with a Prospect that is not an Advisory agreement could fall here ("or otherwise relating to the business").
- **(e)(1)** sets the clock for (a) to (c)(1)(i) records (except the ones (e)(1) lists): "not less than five years from the end of the fiscal year during which the last entry was made on such record, the first two years in an appropriate office of the investment adviser." The clock runs per record, from its last entry. It does not start when a Prospect Household is lost or never converts.
- **(g)(2)(i)**: the adviser must "Arrange and index the records in a way that permits easy location, access, and retrieval of any particular record". (g)(3)(i): to "reasonably safeguard them from loss, alteration, or destruction".
- **Reg S-P** (17 CFR 248). A consumer includes a person who "provides nonpublic personal information to you in connection with obtaining or seeking to obtain brokerage services or investment advisory services, whether or not you provide brokerage services to the individual or establish a continuing relationship with the individual" (248.3(g)(2)(i)), but not one who "provides you only with his or her name, address, and general areas of investment interest in connection with a request for a prospectus, an investment adviser brochure, or other information" (248.3(g)(2)(ii)). A customer needs a "continuing relationship", and an "investment advisory contract" is its example (248.3(j), (k)). The disposal rule requires "reasonable measures" when disposing of "consumer information", and says it does not "require any covered institution to maintain or destroy any record pertaining to an individual that is not imposed under other law" (248.30(b)(1), (b)(3)(i)). The recordkeeping paragraph added to 204-2 in 2024, (a)(25), covers only policies, incident documentation, notification determinations, service-provider contracts, and disposal policies; no per-person data. I did not read the 2024 adopting release.

**What this says for a Prospect.** Rule 204-2 doesn't make the Prospect's Household and People rows a required record. It requires some of what is attached to them: written communications about proposed advice ((a)(7)), advertisements ((a)(11)), written agreements ((a)(10)). (a)(7) and (a)(10) records are kept five years from the end of the fiscal year of their last entry ((e)(1)), and advertisements five years from the end of the fiscal year they were last disseminated ((e)(3)(i)), whether or not the Prospect ever signs. Reg S-P calls a Prospect who gave financial information a consumer and asks for no retention of their data, only proper disposal. Whether a Prospect Household may be deleted therefore turns on whether any such record points at it; and since (g)(2)(i) asks that records be indexed and retrievable, the Household and Person rows are likely how a Firm finds a Prospect's records (my inference, not a source's statement).

## What this says for #192

None of this decides the schema. It maps findings onto the questions #192 asks.

**What `account_role.ends_on` should mean.** Event date or date recorded? The sources point to the event date as `ends_on`, and to the date the Firm recorded it as a second fact kept elsewhere.

- For death, TOD, UTMA and joint survivorship, the law moves ownership on the event (Delaware 807, Maine 1671), so the true end is the date of death or the termination age. The Custodian's registration follows when papers arrive. The glossary's Valid time is this date; ADR 0025's history table, which stamps `valid_until` on the row it replaces, already carries the Record time of the closing.
- For power of attorney, a single date is not enough: Virginia 64.2-1608(D) and Fidelity's form give different dates for the same event. A check of "was this agent authorized when this order was placed" needs both the event date and when the Custodian or the Firm knew.
- The Custodian's account can change identity under a role (a different Account number on a new primary owner), so `ends_on` on an `account_role` row ties to one Account row, and the same event may end a role on one Account and start another on a new Account on the same day.

**Which events end a role and which change it into another.** From the sources:

- _Ends with nothing starting:_ a power of attorney revoked, an agent's death or resignation with no successor, a joint owner leaving when the Account is closed, a beneficiary removed.
- _Ends and another starts on the same Account:_ a surviving spouse who treats an inherited IRA as their own (the `beneficiary` role ends, an `owner` role starts); a custodian's resignation (a new `custodian` role starts); a joint owner's death with survivorship (the survivor's role continues, the Account's Registration type changes at re-registration).
- _Ends on one Account and starts on a new Account (Fidelity's rule, applied by inference):_ a new primary owner, an estate transfer (the old Account is closed), a TOD claim moved into an account in the beneficiary's name (Vanguard), and a UTMA registration change (Fidelity: UTMAs "require a new account number"). A non-spouse's inherited IRA may or may not be a new Account number; the sources don't say.
- _Open:_ the estate, a trust or an entity as holder isn't a Person in the schema, so "the account passes to the estate" has nowhere to point yet. Whether the minor becomes the `owner` of the same Account or a new one depends on the Custodian; Fidelity's UTMA exception suggests a new one but its form doesn't say so for this case.

**Whether `household_member` needs an end date, and what ends it.** The sources describe three practices, and none is the standard.

- Ends by an event on the Person (death) or by moving (divorce, a Dependent who grows up and starts their own Household). Wealthbox keeps the person linked and puts the death on the Person; Redtail and Tamarac CRM remove and relate; Wealthbox moves with no history. Tamarac Reporting's warning, about accounts removed from an account set, is the analogous risk: removing a row changes what a report for a past date shows.
- If membership gets an end date, the schema's two unique indexes span all rows (`unique(household_id, person_id)` and the partial index on `(household_id, role)` for Primary and Co-client). A closed row would block a Person from rejoining and a new Primary from being named. That is a consequence of the current schema, not a finding.
- A date of death looks like a fact about the Person (`person`) in all three CRMs that record it, not about a membership. The surviving spouse takes over as Primary (Redtail, Tamarac) as a role change.

**Whether a Prospect Household may ever be deleted.** Rule 204-2 doesn't require the Prospect Household row itself, and Reg S-P requires no retention of a Prospect's data, only proper disposal. But 204-2(a)(7), (a)(10) and (a)(11) records attached to a Prospect are kept for their own periods ((e)(1), and (e)(3)(i) for advertisements) whether or not the Prospect converts. The rule requires those records, not the Household row: keeping the Household while any such record points at it (a `restrict` foreign key would do this) is a design option, and the records could be kept without it. A Prospect Household with no communication, agreement or advertisement attached is the one case the rule doesn't reach; whether to keep it anyway (#189's "records are kept, never hard-deleted") is a decision for the maintainer, not a finding. Letting a Prospect Household that never converts become a `former_client` is not supported either: the glossary's Former client is "A Household whose Advisory agreement has ended", which a Prospect never had.
