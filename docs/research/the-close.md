# What a custodian does at the end of a business day

> Research notes gathered on 2026-10-30 for issue #48 (the primer on the Close), to settle its gaps with cited sources. They inform the decisions but are not them: #46, #47, #49 and #50 decide the world's rules. Every quote below was read in the cited document unless the text says otherwise. Rule text is eCFR as of 2026-10-28 (read through the versioner API). A claim is "inference" when no source says it.

Terms, once: a **business day** is a day a rule or a firm counts for the clock it runs. The **Close** is the world's routine that finishes a Business day. A **bank holiday** is a day the Federal Reserve Banks are closed. **NAV** is a fund's price per share, worked out from the value of what it holds. **Settlement** is the day cash and shares really change hands after a trade.

## Answer first

1. **End-of-day order.** No public source states an order such as prices, corporate actions, interest, positions, files. The nearest evidence: Rule 17a-3 requires a daily blotter and ledgers, DTCC publishes a timed overnight cycle for clearing, and Schwab says its files hold "close-of-business data". Confidence: high that none of the sources I opened states one, medium that none exists.
2. **Stock and bond calendars differ.** On Columbus Day and Veterans Day the NYSE is open and the bond market and the Federal Reserve are closed. The NYSE was closed on 2025-01-09 and open on 2025-10-13 and 2025-11-11. Confidence: high.
3. **Settlement on those days.** FINRA says Columbus Day and Veterans Day are a business day for Regulation T but "not a settlement date because banking institutions will be closed". No rule defines "business day" for Rule 15c6-1. Confidence: high for FINRA's statement, medium for the Rule 15c6-1 reading.
4. **Bond funds on those days.** Vanguard's bond index funds are open, may "use fair-value pricing on bond market holidays", and Rule 22c-1 lets a fund skip "Customary national business holidays described or listed in the prospectus" (and two other kinds of day). A fund family (Guggenheim) closes its high-yield and long-bond funds. Practice varies. Confidence: high for these two, low for "most funds".
5. **Cash on bank holidays.** FedACH and Fedwire don't process, and Nacha says ACH payments "are not currently settled on weekends and federal holidays". Schwab says its offices open on some bank holidays "are not considered Business Days for purposes relating to the transfer of funds". Confidence: high.
6. **Pay date or month-end on a non-business day.** For Treasuries the rule is documented (next business day, no extra interest). For stock dividends no source read states it. Schwab and Vanguard state monthly interest and dividend timing. Confidence: high for Treasuries and Schwab, not established for dividends.
7. **Corporate actions.** DTC allocates cash and stock distributions on the payable date or later, not on the ex-date. Schwab and Interactive Brokers (IBKR) show the split-adjusted price from the ex-date and new shares on the payable date. Confidence: medium (no custodian states its own posting time of day).
8. **Records aren't rewritten.** Rule 17a-4(f)(2)(i) requires either "a complete time-stamped audit trail" that "will permit re-creation of the original record if it is modified or deleted" or "a non-rewriteable, non-erasable format". It permits modification with a trail, so it doesn't forbid it. Confidence: high.
9. **Catching up after an outage.** FINRA Rule 4370 requires a plan, not a catch-up method. Nothing public says what a custodian does with missed files. Confidence: high that nothing specific is public in what I could open.

## 1. The end of a business day

- **Rule 17a-3(a)(1)** requires "Blotters (or other records of original entry) containing an itemized daily record of all purchases and sales of securities ..., all receipts and deliveries of securities ..., all receipts and disbursements of cash and all other debits and credits." (a)(2) adds "Ledgers (or other records) reflecting all assets and liabilities, income and expense and capital accounts", and (a)(3) a ledger per customer account. It says "daily", not when in the day, and names no order of steps. It supports one record per business day, not a step order.
- **The official close.** NYSE's own page lists "Core Trading Session: 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET" and "4:00 p.m. ET - Closing Auction". NYSE Arca's Rule 1.1(gg) says the Official Closing Price "is the price established in a Closing Auction of one round lot or more", with fallbacks to the last trade and then "the prior trading day's Official Closing Price". I read Arca's rule, not NYSE's own.
- **Funds.** Rule 22c-1(b)(1): NAV "shall be computed no less frequently than once daily, Monday through Friday, at the specific time or times during the day that the board of directors ... sets". The rule sets no time. Vanguard's prospectus sets it: "calculated as of the close of regular trading on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE), generally 4 p.m., Eastern time". Rule 2a-4(a)(2): "Changes in holdings of portfolio securities shall be reflected no later than in the first calculation on the first business day following the trade date." That is a rule on how soon a change must show in the NAV, not an order of steps.
- **Treasury's curve** uses quotes "obtained by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York at or near 3:30 PM each trading day", so a bond price exists before 4 p.m. and is published afterwards.
- **A timed overnight cycle at a clearing agency.** NSCC's Good Friday 2026 notice (A#9732) lists, for the day before: CNS midday projection 5:00 PM ET, "CNS Position Prior to Night Cycle" 9:45 PM, "CNS Balance Order" 10:30 PM, "CNS Night Cycle" 11:30 PM, and output "at normal time - 2:00 AM ET". DTC's Settlement Service Guide: "DTC settlement processing occurs in two cycles: a day cycle and a night cycle. The day cycle runs on the day of settlement. The night cycle begins running the evening of the Business Day prior to the day of settlement." This orders clearing, not a custodian's valuation.
- **Custodian files.** Schwab: "Comprehensive close-of-business data is available daily for: Positions, balances, and transactions; Pricing and security information; Cost basis; Client and account profile information". IBKR: "The statement cutoff time for commodities is generally 5:15 PM EST, and the statement cutoff time for securities is generally 8:20 PM EST", and "Monthly statements are generally available by five business days after the close of a month". Neither orders its steps. **Evidence against any claimed order:** none of these says prices come before corporate actions or interest. The primer's order is the world's design, to be labelled as one.

## 2. Do the stock and bond markets close on the same days?

- **No.** NYSE's 2025 calendar (a PDF; the order of cells is in its colour key, read from the rendered page) marks 2025-01-09 as "Exchange Holiday - Market Closed" and 2025-10-13 and 2025-11-11 with a triangle: "Bank Holidays where NYSE is open. FX and interest rate markets are closed." The 2026 calendar marks 2026-10-12 and 2026-11-11 the same way. NYSE's holiday lists (ICE press release, 2024-11-08, and the live page for 2026 to 2028) name neither Columbus Day nor Veterans Day, which is consistent but only absence.
- **2025-01-09.** ICE (release dated 2024-12-30, ten days ahead; President Carter died on 2024-12-29): NYSE "will close all NYSE Group equity and options markets on Thursday, January 9, 2025, in observance of the National Day of Mourning". SIFMA recommended "an early market close at 2:00 pm EST on January 9, 2025, for all fixed-income cash markets". SIFMA's matrix shows fixed income "OPEN: 2:00 P.M. ET EARLY CLOSE", Fedwire "OPEN", NYSE "CLOSED". So bonds and banks were open and stocks closed: the reverse case.
- **Good Friday** is a second reverse case: NSCC's notice says "both the markets and the National Securities Clearing Corporation (NSCC) will be closed, but U.S. Federal Banks will be open". SIFMA's 2026 list has "Early Close (12:00 p.m. Eastern Time): Friday, April 3, 2026" for bonds, while NYSE is closed all day.
- **SIFMA** (2026 list): "Columbus Day Monday, October 12, 2026", "Veterans Day Wednesday, November 11, 2026". Its recommendations "are recommendations only; each member firm should decide for itself". I did not read SIFMA's 2025 list.
- **Federal Reserve K.8** (2026 to 2030): lists Columbus Day (October 12, 2026) and Veterans Day (November 11, 2026), and "For holidays falling on Saturday, Federal Reserve Banks and Branches will be open the preceding Friday". The page no longer shows 2025.
- **Treasury** publishes a daily par yield curve. Its page says rates are "obtained ... at or near 3:30 PM each trading day" and says nothing of which days it skips. In the 2025 table I read (249 dates), the weekdays with no row are 01-01, 01-20, 02-17, 04-18, 05-26, 06-19, 07-04, 09-01, 10-13, 11-11, 11-27 and 12-25. It has a row, with yields, for 01-09. **Inference:** the Treasury skips the bond-market closes (its gaps match SIFMA-style days, including Columbus Day and Veterans Day, and exclude 01-09). Treasury doesn't state this.
- **Against "one calendar":** the NYSE's own calendar and FINRA's statement treat these as two different clocks. For the world, a stock-market Business day and a bond-market Business day aren't one set (2025-10-13, 2025-11-11 are in one and not the other).

## 3. Settlement on a day the stock market is open but banks are closed

- **Rule 15c6-1(a)** says a broker shall not enter a contract that provides for payment and delivery "later than the first business day after the date of the contract". It doesn't define "business day". SEC's T+1 release (34-96930) uses the term, and gives a compliance date of "Tuesday, May 28, 2024, which follows a Federal holiday for which both markets and banks will be closed". I searched the extracted text for a definition and found none (I did not read every page).
- **FINRA Rule 11120** defines delivery date, ex-date, record date and trade date, not "business day". Rule 11320(b): "delivery shall be made ... on, but not before, the first business day following the date of the transaction". 11320(e): "Contracts due on a day other than a business day shall mature on the next business day."
- **FINRA's schedule** (extensions-of-time page, Q4 2025 to 2026): "The securities exchanges are generally open for trading on Columbus Day and Veterans Day, which are considered a business day under Regulation T of the Federal Reserve Board, SEA Rule 15c3-3 and FINRA Rule 4210(f)(6) and (g)(10)(D). However, Columbus Day and Veterans Day are not settlement dates because banking institutions will be closed on these days". Its table lists "Veterans Day* 11/11/2025", "Columbus Day* 10/12/2026", "Veterans Day* 11/11/2026" as holidays. FINRA Information Notice 11/18/22 says the same.
- **So** a stock trade made on Columbus Day is not settled on Columbus Day, and the next day's count skips it. FINRA doesn't give a T+1 trade-by-trade table in what I read, so exact dates for a trade on the day before are **inference** (settles on the first day after the holiday).
- **DTCC**: the NYSE 2025 calendar's key says "when setting a record date DTCC is closed, and therefore is not counted as a settlement date". Search results described DTCC notices (A#9648, A#9660) saying NSCC is open on Veterans Day with no settlement. I couldn't open those notices (see open items), so they are not relied on.
- **Schwab** defines its Business Day this way: "any day that the New York Stock Exchange and the Federal Reserve Bank of New York are open", so one firm's definition needs both.

## 4. Bond funds, money market funds and sweeps on such a day

- **Rule 22c-1(b)(1)(iii)** lets a fund skip computing NAV on "Customary national business holidays described or listed in the prospectus". Rule 2a-7(a)(4) for money market funds: "Business day means any day, other than Saturday, Sunday, or any customary business holiday."
- **Vanguard Bond Index Funds** (485BPOS, 2022-04-29): NAV "on each day that the NYSE is open for business (a business day)"; "On U.S. holidays or other days when the NYSE is closed, the NAV is not calculated, and the Funds do not sell or redeem shares"; "A fund also may use fair-value pricing on bond market holidays when the fund is open for business (such as Columbus Day and Veterans Day)." So that fund calculates a NAV on those days, from fair-value prices. It doesn't say how a fair value is set.
- **Guggenheim** (2026 holiday page): High Yield, Government Long Bond and Emerging Markets Bond Strategy funds "will also be CLOSED on:" Columbus Day (October 12) and Veterans Day (November 11). "Due to the bank holiday, Money Market will be closed to same-day purchases and redemptions (next-day settlement and exchanges are allowed) on Columbus Day and Veterans Day." So a fund family can choose to close bond funds on bond holidays: no single practice.
- **Schwab sweep:** "Free Credit Balance ... on any Business Day will be swept into your Schwab Sweep Money Fund and generally will not begin earning dividends until the following Business Day." Its definition of Business Day (Q3) means no sweep movement on Columbus Day or Veterans Day. Nothing read says what a bond fund's NAV or a sweep rate uses on a bond holiday beyond Vanguard's fair-value sentence. **Evidence against "a bond fund prices off the last yield":** Vanguard says fair value, not carried-forward.
- **Not established:** iShares, Fidelity and SPDR bond ETF or fund language. I found no primary page that states what a bond ETF's NAV does on those days.

## 5. Cash on bank holidays

- **FedACH** (2026 schedule): for Columbus Day, "Processing Ends Oct. 10, 2026, 3 a.m. ET; Processing Resumes Oct. 12, 2026, 5:30 p.m. ET"; for Veterans Day, "Nov. 10, 2026, 11:30 p.m. ET" to "Nov. 11, 2026, 5:30 p.m. ET". Fedwire: "The Fedwire Funds Service business day ... ends at 7:00 p.m. ET, Monday through Friday, excluding designated holidays". The Fed announced it will add "Sundays and weekday holidays", "implemented in 2028 or 2029".
- **Nacha** fact sheet: "Payments are settled during times when the Federal Reserve's National Settlement Service (NSS) is open, meaning that payments are not currently settled on weekends and federal holidays." "Bill payments that are due over a weekend or holiday are collected the next banking day". A request for comment (responses due 2026-10-23) would define Banking Day as "a calendar day other than a Saturday, Sunday, or Federal banking holiday in the United States", effective "June 18, 2027" if adopted. It's a proposal, not a rule.
- **Custodians:** Schwab says "Although our offices are open on certain bank holidays, these days are not considered Business Days for purposes relating to the transfer of funds". Vanguard: wire redemption requests "on a nonbusiness day" leave "by the close of business on the next business day" (for its money market funds). I found no custodian page that lists its deposit and withdrawal handling by holiday.
- **Caveat:** K.8 notes that on some Saturday holidays the Reserve Banks are open while the Board is closed (July 3, 2026), so "Federal Reserve holiday" means the Banks' schedule, which the rails follow. I read no source that says a custodian posts a deposit on a bank holiday.

## 6. Pay date and month-end on a non-business day

- **Treasuries** (31 CFR 356.30(a)): "If any principal or interest payment date is a Saturday, Sunday, or other day on which the Federal Reserve System is not open for business, we will make the payment (without additional interest) on the next business day."
- **Schwab** (Cash Features, October 2026): Bank Sweep "Interest is compounded daily and credited on the 15th of each month (or the following Business Day if the 15th is not a Business Day)"; Schwab One Interest "accrues daily and is credited to your Account on or before the last Business Day of each month". It also says "if you do not accrue at least one daily dividend of $0.01 during a pay period, you will not receive a money market dividend for that period".
- **Vanguard money market funds** (prospectus 2025-12-19): "Income dividends generally are declared daily and distributed monthly." It gives no payment date and no holiday rule.
- **DTC** Distributions Service Guide (as of 2026-05-06): "Stock Distribution events such as stock dividends, splits, and spinoffs are allocated on the ex-date +2 or the payable date, whichever comes later"; exchanges move ex-dates off a closure "to the next business day that the exchange is open". It has no text I found on a cash payable date that falls on a non-business day.
- **When money starts to earn.** Schwab (Cash Features, October 2026): "Interest accrues on your funds from the day they are deposited at the Program Bank, not while they are held by Schwab". Navy Federal Investment Services' sweep terms (read in the [published .docx copy](https://www.navyfederal.org/content/dam/nfculibs/pdfs/nffg/pershing-sweep-terms-conditions.docx), since the PDF's text can't be extracted): "Interest begins to accrue on the date of deposit within the Program Bank(s), through the business day preceding the date of withdrawal from a Deposit Account." So the date that counts is the deposit at the bank, not the day money reaches the customer's account. **Against "money earns from the day it arrives":** these two, and Schwab's Sweep Money Fund sentence in section 4. That rule, which the world uses, is a simplification (#47).
- **So:** interest accrues every calendar day (Schwab "accrues daily") and is credited on a business day. Which day for a dividend whose Pay date is a holiday: not established. **Inference:** the next business day, by analogy to Treasuries and Schwab.

## 7. Corporate actions

- **What they are:** FINRA 6490(a)(2) lists "dividends or other distributions in cash or kind, stock splits or reverse stock splits, or rights or other subscription offerings", and "Other Company-Related Actions" such as "mergers, acquisitions, dissolutions or other company control transactions". Rule 10b-17(a) makes failing to give notice of a dividend, split or rights offering a "manipulative or deceptive device"; notice goes to FINRA "no later than 10 days prior to the record date". FINRA 11120(d): "ex-date" is "the date on and after which the security is traded without a specific dividend or distribution".
- **Timing at the custodian chain (DTC):** stock dividends "On the morning of the payable date"; "Stock splits, with ex-distribution beginning on the business day following the payable date: For the split shares on ex-date +1"; cash from agents "received by 3:00 p.m." is allocated about every 20 minutes, later funds "the following morning".
- **Custodians:** Schwab: "Distribution/payable date: Additional shares are credited to shareholders' accounts. Effective date: The stock begins trading at its split-adjusted price." IBKR's lesson on Palo Alto's split: "Record Date: December 12; Payable Date: December 13; Ex-distribution Date: December 16" and holders "received one additional share ... after the market close on Dec 13". So shares credit after the close on the payable date, and the new price starts later, on the ex-date. Neither source says the time of day the custodian posts its own record.
- **Against "posted on the ex-date":** Schwab and IBKR put the new shares on the payable date, which for a forward split (IBKR's example) comes before the ex-date. The sources also disagree among themselves: DTC's table gives "For the split shares on ex-date +1" for a split whose ex-distribution begins the business day after the payable date, so the chain's own posting can come after the customer-facing date. Which of these a custodian follows isn't stated.

## 8. Why closed records aren't rewritten

- **Rule 17a-4(f)(2)(i)** (eCFR): an electronic recordkeeping system must "(A) Preserve a record for the duration of its applicable retention period in a manner that maintains a complete time-stamped audit trail that includes: (1) All modifications to and deletions of the record or any part thereof; (2) The date and time of actions that create, modify, or delete the record; ... (4) Any other information needed to maintain an audit trail ... and will permit re-creation of the original record if it is modified or deleted; or (B) Preserve the records exclusively in a non-rewriteable, non-erasable format". Retention is "not less than 6 years, the first two years in an easily accessible place" for 17a-3(a)(1) to (3) records (17a-4(a)).
- **The 2022 amendments** (SEC fact sheet): "Adding an audit-trail alternative to the existing requirement that broker-dealers preserve electronic records exclusively in a non-rewriteable, non-erasable format". The sheet gives the effective date as 60 days after Federal Register publication, six months for broker-dealers' compliance (the release is 34-96034).
- **What the rule says about correcting:** nothing. It requires either that nothing can be overwritten or that every change is logged with the original recoverable. So the world's "never rewritten" is the stricter of the two options, a design choice the rule allows but does not require. Real custodians' correction practice stays as the earlier primers describe it.

## 9. Staying open and catching up

- **FINRA 4370(a)**: a member "must create and maintain a written business continuity plan identifying procedures relating to an emergency or significant business disruption" that is "reasonably designed to enable the member to meet its existing obligations to customers". (c) lists "Data back-up and recovery (hard copy and electronic)" and "All mission critical systems", which include "the maintenance of customer accounts". It sets no method for missed files or a late cycle.
- **Pershing's disclosure** states "recovery time objective for business resumption ... is four (4) hours or less" and a fallback of written requests for "limited trade-related transactions, cash disbursements, and security transfers". It doesn't describe replaying data.
- **Holiday queues** are documented: NSCC's Labor Day 2026 notice says trade input after 8:00 PM Friday "will be accepted but not processed until normal business operations resume on Tuesday", and after Good Friday "CNS activity normally due for distribution on Thursday night/Friday morning will be available to participants on Friday night/Saturday morning (24 hours later than usual)". These are planned holidays, not outages.
- **Unscheduled closures:** DTC's guide says that after one (it names a national day of mourning) "a listed exchange will typically move ex-dates ... to the next business day that the exchange is open".
- **Not found:** any custodian or advisory platform stating what it does after a missed daily file or a late overnight cycle. IBKR's and Schwab's pages say nothing of it.
