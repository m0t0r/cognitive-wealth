# How platforms show Accounts a client is named on but doesn't own

> Research notes gathered on 2026-10-25 for [#145](https://github.com/m0t0r/cognitive-wealth/issues/145). They report what the platforms do, not what Cognitive Wealth should do; the vocabulary is [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md), and where this note and an ADR disagree, the ADR wins. Every quote below was read on the cited page unless the text says otherwise. Typographic apostrophes are written straight.

The question: a Person who is a member of one Household holds an Account role (`beneficiary`, `power_of_attorney`, `custodian`, trustee, authorized trader, interested party) on an Account that another Household owns. [household-accounts.md](household-accounts.md) settled that such a role doesn't pull the Account into the member's Household totals. This note asks what real platforms do next:

1. Does the client's or Household's page show such an Account at all, and under what label?
2. Does it count in Household totals, net worth, AUM or fee billing, automatically by role or by a manual flag?
3. How is Household membership decided?

It also checks whether advisors' beneficiary reviews cover Accounts where the client is the Beneficiary.

## Answer first

1. **Every platform with a public source that shows a named-only Account does it on purpose: a person creates the role record, association, link or toggle.** Salesforce Financial Services Cloud lists every Financial Account Role a client has on the client's Related tab. Orion has "Associated Accounts", which links an Account that "stays in its native household" to another Household with a reported percentage. Tamarac CRM lets a financial account carry "additional households that are stakeholders" next to its owning Household. Redtail has two toggles on a contact's Accounts page. ([Salesforce](#salesforce-financial-services-cloud-and-practifi), [Orion](#orion), [Tamarac](#tamarac-envestnet), [Redtail](#redtail-and-wealthbox))
2. **No source I read counts an Account in a Household's totals because of a `beneficiary`, power of attorney, trustee or similar role.** Where an Account from another Household does count, a person chose it: Orion by entering a percentage (0% stops it), Tamarac by the per-link "Include in Net Worth" setting, Salesforce by "Primary Group" membership. In Salesforce the client-level AUM rollups are keyed only on the two owner roles ([household-accounts.md Q1](household-accounts.md#q1-owner-and-joint-owner)); the page I read doesn't say how a Beneficiary role is treated at group level.
3. **Orion and Tamarac keep the full Account value on the owning Household and report a share or a flag elsewhere.** Orion: "The account itself still shows its full value", and the other Household's "Percent Reported" is whatever a person typed, "Percentages don't have to total 100%". Tamarac Reporting: "AUM can only be enabled in one Household" (household-accounts.md). One Tamarac CRM page gives a different rule for revenue: its calculator counts fees "regardless of whether the CRM account is designated as the Primary Household/Account or an additional associated household".
4. **Household membership is chosen by a person in every platform that documents it**, except Schwab's retail-style householding, which links by "the same last name at the same home address" and otherwise on request. Salesforce asks for "Primary Group" and "Activities and Objects to Roll Up" on each membership; Wealthbox and Redtail allow one household per person; Orion and Tamarac link Households to Accounts by explicit records.
5. **Fee billing is a separate mechanism from Household membership in three platforms.** Orion has "Related Households" (aggregate value for billing only) and "Account Pays For" (a grandparent's Account pays a grandchild's fee). Advyzon's billing checklist has "Are the accounts in the proper household?" and, as a separate item, "Review who is paying for the new account, if it's not paying for itself." Addepar bills a "billable portfolio", which "can be a household, client, legal entity, or group".
6. **Beneficiary reviews, in every source I read, are about the beneficiaries named on the client's own Accounts.** Tamarac's Beneficiary Report, Redtail's Beneficiary Reports and Capital Group's practice guidance all describe it that way. None of them describes tracking the Accounts where the client is the named Beneficiary. That silence is not proof that nobody does it. ([Beneficiary reviews](#beneficiary-reviews))
7. **Where the sources are silent:** Black Diamond, Fidelity Wealthscape, Schwab Advisor Center, Advisor Engine/Junxure, MoneyGuide, Altruist (on households), Practifi (beyond marketing posts), Advyzon (beyond its billing checklist) and Savvy have no public page I could open that says how named-only Accounts are shown or counted. eMoney and Morningstar Office have only thin or dated pages. See [the last section](#how-these-pages-were-read-and-what-i-couldnt-verify).

## Salesforce Financial Services Cloud, and Practifi

Salesforce is the one platform with a role list that includes the roles in #145. Three separate things carry the word "related" in Salesforce, and they are not the same.

**The role list.** The object reference for the managed package's `FinServ_FinancialAccountRole__c` says it "Represents the role occupied by a person or organizational entity that is involved with a financial account, such as a beneficiary or trustee." There are two record types, "account roles" and "contact roles" (Trailhead: "Choose account roles for institutions ... and contact roles for individuals"), and the `Role__c` picklist differs by record type. For a Contact Role the valid values are Accountant, Beneficial Owner, Beneficiary, Business Manager, Grantor and Trustee. For an Account Role they are Accountant, Additional Owner, Administrator, Advisor, Annuitant, Appoint / Remove Authority, Authorized Agent, Authorized Agent / Beneficial Owner, Authorized Individual, Authorized Signer, Beneficiary, Business Manager, Committee Member, Conservator, Control Person, Corporation, Custodian, Deceased Grantor, Decedent, Discretionary, Foundation, Full Authority, Grantor, Guardian, Insured, Interested Party, Minor Primary Owner, Original Depositor, Other, Power of Attorney, Proprietor, TOD Beneficiary and Trustee, plus the system-managed Primary Owner and Joint Owner. Which list a Person gets depends on how the org stores the Person, and the pages I read don't settle it. Trailhead says contact roles are for individuals, yet its own example gives Rachel Adams, a Person Account client, a Beneficiary role with "Record Type: Account Role" and "Related Account: Rachel Adams (Sample)", and the Data 360 page below says the role belongs to "an organization account or person account". My reading, an inference: a Person Account client can take any Account Role value, Power of Attorney and Custodian included, while a Person stored as a Contact gets the six-value list. Trailhead adds that "You can also add new financial account roles to meet your client or business requirements". The field note: "Don't manually update the role to either primary owner or secondary owner, because these roles are automatically assigned based on the primary owner and joint owner of the corresponding financial account record." Each role has `Active__c`, `StartDate__c` and `EndDate__c`, so roles are dated. ([Salesforce Developers, FinServ_FinancialAccountRole__c](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.financial_services_cloud_object_reference.meta/financial_services_cloud_object_reference/sforce_api_objects_financialaccountrole.htm), read with a headed `agent-browser`.) The newer standard model has the same idea as the Financial Account Party object: "The Financial Account Party DMO represents the role of an organization account or person account related to a financial account." ([Data 360 DMO guide](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/data/data-cloud-dmo-mapping/guide/c360dm-financial-account-party-dmo.html).)

**Does the client's page show a named-only Account? Yes, as a role row, not as an Account list.**

- "On the Related tab of the client profile, you can see all the financial account roles that the client has. The Related list includes the Primary Owner and Joint Owner roles, which are created and updated automatically when you indicate that the client is a primary or joint owner on a financial account record." ([Salesforce Help, Add a Financial Account Role](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=fsc_add_financial_account_role.htm&language=en_US&type=5).) So a Beneficiary role the client holds appears in that list, with its financial account.
- The Trailhead unit shows it from the account's side: "Click through the Related tab and scroll down to the Financial Account Roles section. You'll find that while Nigel and Rachel are joint owners—primary and secondary, respectively—Neil Symonds is a beneficiary." Neil is Rachel's father, and in the sample data he heads his own household. The unit doesn't say whether Neil's page or his household's page shows Nigel's account. ([Trailhead, Explore Financial Account Roles and Financial Goals](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/explore-financial-account-roles-and-financial-goals), read in the [Wayback copy of 2023-08-31](https://web.archive.org/web/20230831075026/https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/explore-financial-account-roles-and-financial-goals).)
- A sample Classic Service Console setup for the Account (Individual) page layout lists "Financials (Related Lists: Financial Account Primary Owner, Financial Account Joint Owner, Client Financial Holdings, Client Assets and Liabilities)". No related list for Beneficiary is named there, which is a sample step, not the full layout. ([Salesforce Help, Getting Started: Financial Services with Service in Salesforce](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=000383521&language=en_US&type=1).)
- The rollup text is owner-keyed: "Client-level lookup on Financial Account Role objects. Lookup Field is set to PrimaryOwner__c for the primary owner lookup and JointOwner__c for the joint owner lookups", and for a role object "the Field to Rollup From is on the financial account records related to the financial account role record." (My reading, an inference: the client-level rollup of roles follows the accounts the client owns.) ([Salesforce Help, Rollup By Lookup Configuration and Criteria Fields](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?language=en_US&id=sf.fsc_admin_rbl_configurations.htm&type=5).)

**Does it count?** Totals follow ownership and the Primary Group, as [household-accounts.md Q1](household-accounts.md#q1-owner-and-joint-owner) records. Two further lines from the Trailhead record-rollups units:

- A person can be in several households but only one is the primary group their accounts roll up to: "The Adams household, which includes her husband Nigel Adams's accounts. This is Rachel's primary group, and she's also the primary member of this group. The Symonds household, which includes her father Neil's financial accounts. This is Rachel's secondary group and her father is the group's primary member." And: "She can be a member of another household, such as her parents' household, but her accounts don't roll up there." ([Meet Record Rollups](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/record-rollups-in-financial-services-cloud/meet-record-rollups) and [Map Client Relationships](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/map-client-relationships), both read through the Wayback `web/2025/` copies; the live record-rollups page returned 404 to WebFetch.)
- Group-level rollup of "Financial Account Role" is listed as a supported object with its own view: "Financial Account List, Financial Account Role, Bank Account, Investment Account, Insurance Policy". Whether a member's Beneficiary-only role appears in that group-level role list isn't stated. ([Meet Record Rollups](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/record-rollups-in-financial-services-cloud/meet-record-rollups).)

**"Related accounts" in Salesforce are not Financial Accounts.** The household page has a "Related accounts" section: "Related accounts (3)—Accounts not directly included in her primary household. In this case, it's the Adams Charitable Trust." These are Account records for a business, trust or another household. The Help page: "You can associate a business, trust, or another household only with a household entity, not with a client. To track a client's relationship with a business, trust, or another household, associate the entity with the client's household." ([Map Client Relationships](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/map-client-relationships); [Salesforce Help, Relate a Business, Trust, or Another Household to a Household (Managed Package)](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=ind.fsc_relate_account.htm&language=en_US&type=5).) Their money rolls up only if the flag is set: "Unless a related account or contact lists the household as their primary household, their accounts won't roll up there."

**How membership is decided.** By hand, on the Relationships tab: "Role in Group: Client", "Primary Member: Enabled", "Primary Group: Enabled", "Activities and Objects to Roll Up: All". "Each household can only have one primary member." ([Map Client Relationships](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/client-management-with-financial-services-cloud/map-client-relationships).) None of the Salesforce pages I read ties membership to a financial account role.

**Practifi.** Practifi is a CRM sold for wealth managers. I found no public help centre or object reference. Its own blog says only that a wealth-management CRM "stores a complete profile for every client and household: personal details, financial goals, investment preferences, risk profile, beneficiaries, related relationships, and lifecycle stage", and that its data model has "Household and entity structures, relationship mapping across family members and associated advisors". ([Practifi blog, What Is a CRM?](https://www.practifi.com/blog/what-is-a-crm-a-guide-for-rias-ifas-and-multi-family-offices/) and [Who Is Practifi For?](https://www.practifi.com/blog/who-is-practifi-for/), the vendor's own blog.) Nothing there says how a named-only Account is shown or counted.

## Orion

Orion is the one platform that names the feature in #145 outright and documents how it counts.

- **The model.** "Households are one part of a Portfolio, the other two records being a Registration and an Account." The Registration record carries "Account Type(ie 401K), SSN, BirthDate, Beneficiaries", so a Beneficiary is a field on the Registration, not a link to a Person with a Household of their own. ([Orion Developers Portal, Creating a New Portfolio or Household](https://developers.orionadvisor.com/?p=602).)
- **Associated Accounts.** "Associated Account: An account that stays in its native household but is linked to another Household or Portfolio Group for reporting and ownership." "Associated Registration: A registration that stays in its native household but is linked to another Household or Portfolio Group. All underlying accounts in the registration are included automatically." Where the page puts it: "Open Portfolio Audit and select the Household. Click Edit Household. Select Associated Accounts in the left menu." ([Orion Support, Portfolio View - Ownership](https://orionadvisorservices.my.site.com/OrionSupportApp/s/article/Portfolio-View---Ownership), article dated 2026-10-06, read with a headed `agent-browser`.) Orion's release note introduces it: "Link entire registrations from external households directly to a client's reporting household." The labels "External Accounts" and "External Households" were renamed "Associated" in September 2026 because "'External' was often ambiguous". ([Orion Release Highlights](https://orion.com/orion-release-highlights), September 2026, "Associated Registrations for Reporting (09/25)".)
- **Does it count?** It counts by the percentage a person types. "Percent Reported: The share of an associated account or registration's value that reports to the Household or Portfolio Group. The account itself still shows its full value." "Percentages don't have to total 100%. Enter the share the client or advisor wants to report for each Household or Portfolio Group." "To stop reporting an association, add a 0% history entry instead, so the ownership history stays intact." Percentages carry an "As Of" date, so they are dated like Account roles. Reports, performance and rollups apply the percentage in force for the period. (Same page.)
- **What the page uses it for.** Its examples are ownership, not a named role: "A family trust is shared across three households. How do I show each household's share without duplicating the trust?" and "As beneficiaries receive distributions, historical reports show each beneficiary's percentage throughout the distribution timeline." It says nothing about Beneficiary-while-the-owner-is-alive, power of attorney or custodian roles, and nothing about AUM or billing.
- **Billing is separate.** "Relate households together so Orion combines their values into one aggregate for billing... Aggregation affects the billing value only — each household still reports separately." The example is "a parent and their children". Related Households are linked by hand, and the Billing app's tabs let you relate a Household, Portfolio Group, Registration or Account. ([Orion Support, How to Relate Households](https://orionadvisorservices.my.site.com/OrionSupportApp/s/article/How-to-Relate-Households), article dated 2026-09-30.) A separate payer link: "When an account pays for another account's management fee in Orion, the account set to pay the fee is the 'direct' account, and the account being paid for is the 'indirect' account"; the Billing FAQ's example is "a grandparent pays the bill for their grandchild's college savings account", and the picker offers "an account within this household or a related household", with a box to "search for any account in your database". ([Orion Support, Account Pays For Relationships](https://orionadvisorservices.my.site.com/OrionSupportApp/s/article/Setting-an-Account-to-Pay-for-Another) and [Billing FAQs](https://orionadvisorservices.my.site.com/OrionSupportApp/s/article/Billing-common-questions-FAQ-s).)
- **API.** The public developer page says the Portfolio API covers "Households, Registrations, Financial Account Records, Assets, transactions"; the full API reference needs credentials, so I could not read an owner or beneficiary schema. ([Orion Developers Portal](https://developers.orionadvisor.com/quick-start/).)

## Tamarac (Envestnet)

[household-accounts.md Q1](household-accounts.md#q1-owner-and-joint-owner) covers Tamarac Reporting. Tamarac CRM adds the multi-Household case.

- **Stakeholder Households.** In Tamarac CRM each financial account has "Primary Account/Household: The household that owns the financial account" and an "Account/Household Subgrid" that "Displays all additional households that are stakeholders for the financial account." The page's example is the case in #145: "The grandparents have a 529 account for the children, their household is in the Primary Account/Household field. The parents should also be able to see information about this account, their account can also be associated with the financial account as well." ([Tamarac, Financial Accounts Integration](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_crm/integrations/tamarac_platform_integrations/financial_accounts_integration.htm).) The page doesn't say what a stakeholder's role is, and "stakeholder" is not a Beneficiary or power of attorney role.
- **Does it count?** There is a per-link flag. "Net worth is based on Include in Net Worth in the Tamarac Reporting Household" for linked Households, and "To include a Financial Account in the net worth calculation for a Household, turn on Include in Net Worth for that Financial Account", set on the Household/Financial Account Relationship record. Net worth = "(Managed Values + Unmanaged Values of all associated Financial Accounts with Include in Net Worth = Yes) + Assets - Liabilities". ([Tamarac, Track Managed Value and Net Worth in Tamarac CRM](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_crm/reports/track_managed_value_and_net_worth_in_tamarac_crm.htm).) The CRM revenue calculator is different: it "includes management fees from every financial account that's linked from the Reporting household to the CRM account, regardless of whether the CRM account is designated as the Primary Household/Account or an additional associated household". ([Tamarac, Household Integration](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_crm/integrations/tamarac_platform_integrations/household_integration.htm).)
- **Two Tamarac pages disagree.** The net-worth page's Limitations say "You can only assign the Financial Account to one Account. You can't assign a Financial Account to multiple accounts." The financial-accounts page describes stakeholder Households on the same account. I can't reconcile them from the pages (the first may mean one owning Account, the second additional Households).
- **Membership** is chosen: "If your firm does not use integration to manage financial account membership, there is a one-time assignment that will need to occur for all new financial accounts", done with "Add Existing Financial Account". With the Reporting integration, membership is the Household's membership in Reporting. In Reporting the Household's "Direct Members" tab shows each account and group with an "Include in Net Worth" checkbox. ([Tamarac, Do Not Include an Account or Group in Net Worth for a Household](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_reporting/accounts-groups-households/households/creating_and_maintaining_households/do_not_include_an_account_or_group_in_net_worth_for_a_household.htm).)
- **Ownership Type** is a separate field with three values: "Joint. For accounts held by the entire Household, such as a mortgage or joint brokerage account.", "Individual. For accounts held by one individual, such as a 401(k), IRA, or individual brokerage account.", "Other. For accounts with ownership other than joint or individual, such as a trust account or an educational account." ([Tamarac, Ownership Type](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/data_dictionary/o/ownership_type.htm).) There is no Beneficiary or power of attorney value.

## Redtail and Wealthbox

**Redtail CRM.** Redtail's help centre is public (Knowledge articles).

- An Account is linked to a contact, and the contact's page lists them: "Navigate to an individual contact record's account page to see a list of all accounts the record is linked to." The Accounts page options include "Show Family Accounts - Display all accounts for the contact and contacts related to the contact (such as a spouse or dependent)." and "Show Non-primary Joint Accounts - Display all accounts where the contact is the joint owner." An account can be moved: "Move Account - Move the account to another contact's record." Beneficiaries are entered on a different screen: "View Account Details - Takes you to a more detailed page about the account, where you can add Beneficiaries". And the page shows "a total between all accounts at the bottom of the page" for Face Value and Balance. ([Redtail, Contact Overview Menu: Accounts](https://support.redtailtechnology.com/s/article/Contact-Overview-Menu-Accounts), dated 2024-11-01, read with a headed `agent-browser`.)
- My reading, an inference: the second toggle's name means joint accounts where the contact is not the primary record are hidden until you switch it on. The page doesn't say whether the total changes when the toggle does.
- Membership is chosen and exclusive: "your individual contacts can only be linked to one family". Spouses and tax dependants go in a Family under one Head of Household; people outside it go under Memberships: "If the child or grandchild is grown with a family of their own... you should link them via Memberships." ([Redtail, Defining Family Relationships](https://support.redtailtechnology.com/s/article/Defining-Family-Relationships) and [Householding Recommendations](https://support.redtailtechnology.com/s/article/Householding-Recommendations), read with a headed `agent-browser`.)
- No Redtail page I opened has a power of attorney, trustee or custodian role on an Account.

**Wealthbox.** Wealthbox has no Account model in the pages I read; the only place those pages show Accounts is a portfolio-system integration tab. Its households: "In Wealthbox, Household records help organize multiple related contacts—like family members at the same address—under one shared record." "Household Members vs Related Contacts: Same address contacts, those living under the same roof should be linked under 'Household Members.' Related contacts can be used to relate contact records that are not under the same address. For example, adult children." And, in its FAQ: "No, a person contact can only be associated with one household at a time in Wealthbox." ([Wealthbox Help, What are Household Records and their best practices](https://help.wealthbox.com/hc/en-us/articles/29980360361755-What-are-Household-Records-and-their-best-practices), read with a headed `agent-browser`.) Its Black Diamond integration page: "Accounts linked to a contact with a valid email will appear in Wealthbox. Users can also manually link accounts if necessary" and, for Households, "it feeds to both 'Person' and 'Household' Contact Records under the 'Black Diamond' tab". ([Wealthbox Help, The Black Diamond Integration](https://help.wealthbox.com/hc/en-us/articles/29980410862875-The-Black-Diamond-Integration).) My reading, an inference: a named-only Account would show in Wealthbox if Black Diamond links it to that contact; the pages don't say how Black Diamond treats such Accounts.

## Addepar

Addepar's developer documentation is public. It models ownership as a graph, with no role for a Beneficiary or an agent.

- "An ownership structure is a hierarchy of households, clients, legal entities, accounts, and investments." "Households can own all parts of the portfolio. Clients can own all parts of the portfolio except for households." "A position connects each part of the hierarchy... it holds information like value, percent of ownership, and inception date." "A client can also share ownership of a legal entity, account, or investment with another person. This is called joint ownership." ([Addepar 101](https://developers.addepar.com/docs/about-addepar.md).)
- A Position carries "incepting_open_position_ownership_percentage: Percentage ownership. Number. (Percent, represented as a decimal)", and the Household model type is percent-based. ([Positions](https://developers.addepar.com/docs/positions.md) and [Model Types](https://developers.addepar.com/docs/model-types.md).)
- Billing: "A billable portfolio is a portfolio that is associated with a specific fee schedule. A billable portfolio can be a household, client, legal entity, or group." ([Billable Portfolios](https://developers.addepar.com/docs/billing.md).)
- Read with the docs' own `.md` copies. A named-only Account would appear in a Household only if someone creates a Position with some percentage, which is how Orion's Associated Account behaves. Addepar's page doesn't say so; that is my comparison. The Help Center article on the 2024 "household entity type" wasn't reachable.

## Advyzon, eMoney, Morningstar Office

- **Advyzon.** Its billing checklist (the vendor's PDF) asks, for new accounts: "Are the accounts in the proper household?" and "Review who is paying for the new account, if it's not paying for itself." ([Advyzon, Billing & Reporting Checklist](https://main.advyzon.com/main/viewpoints/images/Billing%20&%20Reporting%20Checklist.pdf).) So Advyzon has a payer notion apart from the Account's own Household. Its help centre wasn't reachable, so I don't know how a named-only Account is shown.
- **eMoney.** The Fidelity integration Q&A (eMoney's own blog PDF, 2016) says "Advisors can easily create a Household view in eMoney, linking both a client and spouse through the integration" and that after linking the primary client, "the spouse can also be linked to the household in eMoney and their accounts will also be imported" (the PDF's text breaks the last word). Nothing on non-owner roles. ([eMoney, Fidelity Integration Q&A](https://blog.emoneyadvisor.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/FidelityIntegration-QandA.pdf).) The developer pages I opened don't describe the data model.
- **Morningstar Office.** A 2011 vendor tutorial says that after merging family members into one client, "In the Accounts screen, you'll also see the accounts of all members listed", and that removing a member moves their accounts: "you'll see that all of the member's accounts have been moved into the new Client as well." That is membership-driven, and old. ([Morningstar, Merging and Un-Merging Clients in Morningstar Office, 2011](https://gladmainnew.morningstar.com/articles/tutorial/30/Merging_and_Un-Merging_Clients_in_Morningstar_Office[1].pdf).) I couldn't open the product's current training index, so a "billing group" feature that a search summary mentioned is not verified.

## Schwab Advisor Services

Schwab's published householding is for fee waivers and commissions on Schwab's side, not an advisor's reporting household. Its pricing guide for clients of independent advisors:

- "'Accounts of Your Household' are accounts that have been linked in accordance with Schwab's householding guidelines. Schwab makes a reasonable effort to automatically link qualified accounts of persons with the same last name at the same home address".
- "Schwab may consider other accounts to be in your household upon your request if account holders are in the same family, if there is a dependent relationship, or in certain other similar instances at Schwab's discretion."
- Under "Rules Applicable to Fiduciary Accounts": "IRA, Custodial, and certain Trust accounts that meet Schwab's householding guidelines generally will be included in Household Calculations. If you have an IRA, Custodial, or Trust account and you are acting in a fiduciary capacity, it is your responsibility and not Schwab's to consider whether it is appropriate to consider the balances and activities of that account in Household Calculations, which could benefit the fiduciary account and/or other Accounts of Your Household. You must contact Schwab to request that the balances and activities of your fiduciary account not be included in Household Calculations if you determine that it is not appropriate."

([Schwab, Pricing Guide for Advisor Services](https://www.schwab.com/legal/schwab-pricing-guide-for-advisor-services), read with a headed `agent-browser`.) This is close to the retail guide quoted in household-accounts.md Q3 but not word for word (the retail text says such accounts "will be automatically included", this one says "generally will be included"). I found no public page on how Schwab Advisor Center shows an advisor's households or related accounts.

## Black Diamond, Fidelity, Altruist, AdvisorEngine/Junxure, MoneyGuide, Savvy

- **Black Diamond (SS&C).** The only vendor page I could read is the API product brief, which lists under "Household Information": "Client Relationships (Households)", "Contacts", "Portfolios", "Client Portal Users". No help article opened. ([SS&C Black Diamond API product brief](https://www.sscblackdiamond.com/media/vvigpui2/ssc-black-diamond-product-brief-api.pdf), text extracted with pypdf.) No public source.

## Beneficiary reviews

The question: do advisors track Accounts where the client is the Beneficiary, or only beneficiaries on the client's own Accounts?

- Tamarac CRM's Beneficiary Report shows beneficiary data for a selected Household, covering the financial accounts, trusts and life insurance policies associated with it, and lists records flagged "Requires Beneficiary" that have no beneficiary, so gaps show. The page doesn't mention Accounts where the client is the Beneficiary. ([Tamarac, Beneficiary Report](https://help.tamaracinc.com/help/content/tamarac_crm/reports/beneficiary_report.htm), read in full with `curl`; the description is a paraphrase.)
- Redtail's Accounts page lists "Beneficiary Reports" among its reports and adds beneficiaries from an Account's detail page (quoted above).
- Capital Group's practice guidance tells advisors to "Conduct a beneficiary review": "Order a report listing the beneficiaries for your client's IRAs" and "Send your client a beneficiary review letter and follow up with phone calls", and "Ask for introductions to the beneficiaries when appropriate." ([Capital Group, Manage individual retirement accounts](https://www.capitalgroup.com/advisor/practicelab/articles/manage-ira-accounts.html), read with a headed `agent-browser`.) That is a review of designations on the client's Accounts, and the beneficiaries are prospects.
- Altruist keeps beneficiaries per Account and lets advisors reuse and edit them in the Advisor Portal (above).

I found no regulator or custodian page that sets a practice either way. The sources describe beneficiary review as maintaining designations on Accounts the client owns, not as a list of Accounts the client will inherit.

## What this says for Cognitive Wealth

What the evidence supports:

- **Showing a named-only Account without counting it is a real pattern.** Salesforce's role list on the client page, Tamarac's "in the Household, out of net worth" and Orion's Associated Account at a typed percentage all show an Account to someone who doesn't own it. Orion's Associated Accounts and Tamarac CRM's stakeholder Households are built for a second Household's page; Salesforce's is a role list on the Person.
- **No platform decides it by role.** Where an Account from another Household is counted anywhere, a person set the link, a flag or a percentage. That agrees with household-accounts.md rule 4 and 5 (a `beneficiary` or `power_of_attorney` role doesn't make an Account part of a Household).
- **The owner keeps the whole Account.** Orion and Tamarac Reporting both leave the owning Household's value intact and put a share or a flag on the other side. Salesforce lets a person be a member of several households while only the Primary Group rolls up.
- **Fee billing and display are separate settings** (Orion, Advyzon, Addepar). Showing an Account in a Household's overview doesn't have to decide who pays for it.
- **Beneficiary reviews in the sources look at the client's own Accounts.** A related-Accounts section is not what those reviews use.

Still a design choice, because no source settles it:

- Whether the Household overview should show the Accounts a member is only named on at all. Salesforce does it on the Person, Orion and Tamarac only after someone links an Account by hand.
- Whether the section is on the Household page or the Person page, and what it lists (the role row alone, or the Registration type and masked number as #145 proposes).
- Which roles count as "named on": only Salesforce publishes a role list, and its list depends on the record type (a Contact Role offers Beneficiary, Trustee, Beneficial Owner, Grantor, Accountant, Business Manager; an Account Role adds Interested Party, Authorized Signer, Power of Attorney, Custodian and more). Whether a Person gets the short or the long list depends on how the org stores Persons. No source I read has an authorized trader role.
- A custodian and a minor in different Households; joint owners in two Households; a per-Account override. Orion's typed percentage and Tamarac's per-link flag are the closest existing precedents, and neither is role-driven.
- Whether related Accounts may ever count toward AUM or fees. Orion lets a firm do it by percentage; no source lets a role do it.
